\section{Scope and Purpose of Hazard Analysis} \label{SecScopePurpose}

% Instructor prompt (hidden): You should say what loss could be incurred
% because of the hazards.

\progname{} is designed to change how its users behave: to be more active, to
keep up a routine, and to make investing decisions. Features that change
behaviour can also cause harm, even when they work as intended. For example,
one interview participant described a step streak that now makes them go out
walking later than they should (P01). The purpose of this
hazard analysis is to identify how \progname{} could cause harm and to define
requirements that control it. Hazards are identified using Failure Mode and
Effect Analysis (FMEA) in Section~5, and the resulting safety and security
requirements are listed in Section~\ref{SecSafetyReqs} and added to the SRS.
The aim is to reduce each risk as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP).

In-app currency has no real-world value, so \progname{} cannot cause a direct
financial loss. However, given it is expected to simulate real-world trading it can influence a user's financial choices outside the app. Table~\ref{TblLosses} lists the losses it can cause. Each requirement in
Section~\ref{SecSafetyReqs} names the losses it controls.

\textbf{ID} & \textbf{Loss} & \textbf{Description}\\
\midrule
L1 & Physical harm & Injury or unsafe exercise, such as exercising while sick

\addlinespace

The analysis covers the \progname{} mobile app on Android and iOS, its server
and database, and its use of the phone's health platform and the market data
provider. Hazards are considered both when a part fails and when a feature
behaves as intended. The following are out of scope:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The internal workings of the health platforms and the market data
  provider. How their failures appear in \progname{}, such as missing activity
  data or delayed prices, is in scope.
  \item Injuries that are part of a sport or activity itself.
  \item Stretch goals (Problem Statement Section~3). They will be analysed if
  they are built.
\end{itemize}

The requirements in this section control the hazards identified in the Failure
Mode and Effect Analysis (Section~5).
Each requirement states what \progname{} shall do, how a tester decides whether
it is met (its fit criterion), why it is needed, which hazards and losses it
controls, and where it appears in the Software Requirements Specification
(SRS). Every requirement except SAF-5 appears in the SRS with the same
identifier and wording. SAF-5 applies only if goal suggestions are added. Values written as \texttt{SMALL\_CAPS} constants are defined in SRS

\subsection{Physical and Psychological Well-being}

\begin{description}

\hazreq{SAF-1}{Rest days protect streaks}
{The system shall let the user mark up to \texttt{REST\_DAYS\_PER\_PERIOD}
days in each \texttt{STREAK\_PERIOD} as rest days, and a rest day shall not end
the user's activity streak.}
{A test user with a streak who marks a day as a rest day and records no
activity that day does not have the streak reset to zero at the start of the
following day. A request to mark more than \texttt{REST\_DAYS\_PER\_PERIOD}
rest days in one \texttt{STREAK\_PERIOD} is refused.}
{Streaks reward consistency, which can lead users to exercise while sick,
injured or late at night to keep a streak alive. In user interviews, one
participant described a step streak that makes them go out walking when they
otherwise would not, including late in the evening (P01), and another admired
pushing through exercise regardless of pain (P02).}
{FMEA H9, L1 (physical harm), L2 (psychological harm).}
{SRS Section~6.2, FR\_ActRestDay.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SAF-2}{No penalty for a lapse}
{The system shall not reduce a user's virtual currency balance or simulated
holdings when they miss an activity goal or their streak ends, and shall not
show a missed goal or an ended streak to other users.}
{For a test user who misses their goal on day \textit{d}, the currency balance
and simulated holdings at the start of day \textit{d+1} equal those at the end
of day \textit{d}. No friend's social feed shows the missed goal or the ended
streak.}
{The only consequence of missing a goal is the end of the streak, unless the
day is a rest day (\reqref{SAF-1}). Activity recorded on that day still earns
currency, as it would with no goal set (\reqref{SAF-3}). Interviews show that
keeping a streak going is already a strong motivator (P01). Comparison becomes
harmful mainly when a user falls far behind (P02). Taking away earned currency
or holdings would also add pressure to exercise when unwell, and would mix
fitness penalties into the investment results the user is trying to learn
from.}
{FMEA H9, L1 (physical harm), L2 (psychological harm), L6 (financial-understanding harm).}
{SRS Section~6.2, FR\_ActNoPenalty.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SAF-3}{Goals are optional}
{The system shall not require the user to set an activity goal in order to earn
currency for recorded activity.}
{A test user with no activity goal set who records activity receives currency
for it.}
{Some users track their activity informally and set no goals (P02). Requiring a
goal would push them into targets they did not choose, or shut them out of the
reward that is central to \progname{}.}
{FMEA H9, L1 (physical harm), L2 (psychological harm).}
{SRS Section~6.2, FR\_ActGoalOptional.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SAF-4}{No automatic goal changes}
{The system shall not change a user's activity goal without the user's
confirmation.}
{A test user who exceeds their goal on \texttt{GOAL\_REVIEW\_PERIOD}
consecutive days still has the same goal unless they confirm a change.}
{Users fit their goals around their available time and energy. One participant
deliberately keeps their step goal fixed, even though they regularly exceed it,
because a higher goal takes too much time (P01). Raising goals automatically
ignores these limits and leads to overexertion or to goals that are repeatedly
missed. Users can still change their own goal at any time (SRS Section~6.2,
FR\_ActGoal). A change applies from the next day, so lowering a goal cannot
keep the current day's streak alive.}
{FMEA H9, L1 (physical harm), L2 (psychological harm).}
{SRS Section~6.2, FR\_ActNoAutoGoal.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SAF-5}{Bounded goal suggestions}
{The system shall not suggest increasing a user's activity goal by more than
\texttt{MAX\_GOAL\_INCREASE} in any one-week period.}
{Over any 7-day period, every goal the system suggests to a test user is at
most the user's goal at the start of that period multiplied by
(1 + \texttt{MAX\_GOAL\_INCREASE}).}
{Large jumps in a goal are not wanted. When raising a challenge, one participant
does so only until it is hard but still winnable, and keeps their step goal
fixed because a higher target takes too much time (P01). The system therefore
limits suggested increases to \texttt{MAX\_GOAL\_INCREASE} per week.}
{FMEA H9, L1 (physical harm).}
{None. \progname{} does not suggest goal changes in the capstone.}
{Future, if goal suggestions are added.}

\hazreq{SAF-6}{Reminders only in chosen periods}
{The system shall send reminders of the types in Table~\ref{TblReminderTypes}
only during the time periods the user has chosen to receive them.}
{For a test user who has chosen their reminder periods, no reminder of any type
in Table~\ref{TblReminderTypes} is delivered outside those periods. For a test
user who has not chosen any periods, reminders are delivered only during
\texttt{DEFAULT\_REMINDER\_PERIOD}.}
{A reminder is only useful when the user can act on it. During times such as
working hours, a reminder to be active or to check a portfolio cannot be acted
on and only interrupts the user. Late reminders also add pressure to exercise
late at night: one participant described their streak making them go out
walking at 9 p.m.\ when they otherwise would not (P01). Letting users choose
when they are reminded keeps reminders useful and avoids late-night pressure.}
{FMEA H9, L1 (physical harm), L2 (psychological harm).}
{SRS Section~6.6, FR\_RemindInPeriods. Implemented in part by SRS
Section~6.6, FR\_RemindPeriods.}
{Capstone.}

\end{description}

\subsection{Activity Data Integrity}

\begin{description}

\hazreq{SAF-7}{Daily earning cap}
{The system shall award a user no more than \texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_CURRENCY}
virtual currency for recorded activity in any calendar day.}
{For a test user whose recorded activity in one calendar day is plausible and
would otherwise earn more than \texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_CURRENCY}, the balance increases by exactly
\texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_CURRENCY} for that day. Each activity counts toward the
calendar day in the user's time zone at the time of the activity.}
{Without a cap, more activity always earns more currency, which rewards
exercising past a healthy amount. One participant admired pushing through
exercise regardless of pain (P02). A cap also limits what non-exercise motion
can earn: phones count steps while riding a bus, and one tracking app was left
running for hours after a ride had ended (P01). The cap applies to currency
rather than to any one activity, so it works the same way for every supported
activity type (Problem Statement Goal 5). The cap limits how much a day can
earn, but it does not judge whether an activity record is genuine.
\reqref{SEC-1} covers that.}
{FMEA H2, H6, H8, H9, L1 (physical harm), L4 (security loss), L5 (data loss and
integrity).}
{SRS Section~6.2, FR\_ActDailyCap.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-1}{Implausible activity earns nothing}
{The system shall not count an activity record that exceeds the plausibility
limits for its activity type toward the user's currency, activity trends,
streak or milestones, and shall show the user that the record was not counted.}
{A test record of more than \texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_STEPS} steps in one day, or a
workout longer than \texttt{MAX\_WORKOUT\_DURATION}, earns no currency, does
not appear in the user's activity trends, streak or milestones, and is shown to
the user as not counted.}
{\reqref{SAF-7} limits how much currency a day can earn, but it does not judge
whether a record is genuine. Under the cap alone, a fabricated record still
earns the full daily amount. An accidental one, such as a tracking app left
running for hours after a ride had ended (P01), also distorts the user's
activity trends, which the cap does not touch. This requirement rejects records
that could not come from real activity.
Small amounts of everyday tracking noise, such as steps counted while riding a
bus (P01), stay within the limits and are accepted. The limits target records
that are clearly impossible.}
{FMEA H2, H6, L4 (security loss), L5 (data loss and integrity).}
{SRS Section~6.2, FR\_ActPlausible.}
{Capstone.}

\end{description}

\subsection{Financial Understanding}

\begin{description}

\hazreq{SAF-8}{Simulation notice before the first trade}
{Before the user's first trade, the system shall display a notice stating that
the market is simulated, that virtual currency and holdings have no real-world
value, and that nothing in \progname{} is financial advice. The system shall
not complete the first trade until the user acknowledges the notice.}
{A new test user who attempts their first trade is shown the notice and cannot
complete the trade until they acknowledge it. The notice contains all three
statements: the market is simulated, currency and holdings have no real-world
value, and nothing in \progname{} is financial advice.}
{\progname{} teaches investing by letting users practise, and people learn how
to act from what they practise: one participant said that formal study did not
show them how to act until they had hands-on experience (P02). The lessons a
simulation teaches can carry over to real decisions, and a user could
mistake simulated results or in-app content for advice on what to do with real
money. Users who learn investing on their own also draw on informal sources of
mixed quality: one participant learned from online videos and forums, warned
that it is easy to end up following the wrong kinds of investing content, and
looked for answers tailored to their own situation (P01).}
{FMEA H22, L6 (financial-understanding harm).}
{SRS Section~6.3, FR\_MktSimNotice.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SAF-9}{No personalized recommendations}
{The system shall not recommend that a user buy or sell a specific asset.}
{No screen or notification shown to a test user tells them to buy or sell a
named asset, or presents assets as a ``recommended pick''.}
{Recommending specific assets would be personalized financial advice, which
\progname{} is not licensed to give, and users may act on it with real money
(P01).}
{FMEA H22, L6 (financial-understanding harm).}
{SRS Section~6.3, FR\_MktNoRecommend. Implemented in part by SRS
Section~6.4, FR\_LearnConcentration.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SAF-10}{Label simulated returns}
{The system shall label every displayed return and portfolio value as
simulated.}
{Every screen that shows a return or portfolio value shows a ``Simulated''
label next to it.}
{Simulated returns leave out real costs such as fees and taxes, so users could
mistake them for results they would get with real money.}
{FMEA H22, L6 (financial-understanding harm).}
{SRS Section~6.3, FR\_MktLabelReturns.}
{Capstone.}

\end{description}

\subsection{Market Price and Trade Integrity}

\begin{description}

\hazreq{SEC-2}{Price freshness}
{The system shall display each asset price with the time it was obtained, and
shall mark the price as delayed when it is older than
\texttt{QUOTE\_STALENESS\_LIMIT}.}
{A test price obtained more than \texttt{QUOTE\_STALENESS\_LIMIT} ago is shown
with its time and a ``Delayed'' marker. A price obtained within the limit is
shown with its time and no marker.}
{Market data request limits mean prices can lag the real market. A lagging
price shown as current misrepresents how the market moved and how a trade
performed. The limit matches Problem Statement Goal 4 (prices within 15 minutes
of the real market).}
{FMEA H12, H45, L5 (data loss and integrity), L6 (financial-understanding
harm).}
{SRS Section~6.3, FR\_MktPriceAge.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-3}{No trades on stale prices}
{The system shall not execute a trade while the asset's market is open and the
asset's price is older than \texttt{QUOTE\_STALENESS\_LIMIT}.}
{While a test asset's market is open and its price is older than
\texttt{QUOTE\_STALENESS\_LIMIT}, a trade request for it is refused and the
user is told the price is out of date. With a price within the limit, the trade
proceeds.}
{A trade at an old price misrepresents how the market moved and teaches the
wrong lesson. The limit is the same one \reqref{SEC-2} uses to mark a price as
delayed.}
{FMEA H12, L5 (data loss and integrity), L6 (financial-understanding harm).}
{SRS Section~6.3, FR\_MktNoStaleTrade.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-4}{Queue trades while the market is closed}
{The system shall queue a trade requested while the asset's market is closed,
and execute it at the first price obtained after the market next opens.}
{A test trade requested while the market is closed is shown to the user as
queued, with a notice that it will run at the opening price, and is executed at
the first price obtained after the market opens.}
{Many users have time to practise in the evenings and on weekends, when markets
are closed. Running those trades at the last closing price would use an old
price, and refusing them without explanation would confuse the user.}
{FMEA H18, L5 (data loss and integrity), L6 (financial-understanding harm).}
{SRS Section~6.3, FR\_MktQueueClosed.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-5}{Complete or reject trades}
{The system shall record each trade completely, updating both the currency
balance and the holdings, or not at all. It shall reject a purchase that costs
more than the user's currency balance and a sale of more shares than the user
holds.}
{A test trade interrupted before it completes leaves the balance and holdings
unchanged, and a completed trade changes both. A purchase costing more than the
balance, or a sale of more shares than are held, is rejected and changes
neither.}
{A partly recorded trade could take currency without adding holdings, or the
reverse. Allowing a user to spend more than their balance or sell more than
they hold would create currency or holdings that were never earned.}
{FMEA H19, H20, H44, L5 (data loss and integrity).}
{SRS Section~6.3, FR\_MktAtomicTrade.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-6}{No duplicate trades}
{The system shall record a trade only once when the same trade request is
submitted more than once.}
{When a test user submits a trade and the same request is sent again, for
example by retrying after a dropped connection, exactly one trade is recorded.}
{Mobile connections drop, and an app or user may resend a trade that already
went through. Recording it twice would change the balance and holdings in a way
the user did not intend.}
{FMEA H16, H44, L5 (data loss and integrity).}
{SRS Section~6.3, FR\_MktNoDuplicate.}
{Capstone.}

\end{description}

\subsection{Privacy and Permissions}

\begin{description}

\hazreq{SEC-7}{Least-privilege health access}
{The system shall request read access only to the health data types listed as
activity inputs in the SRS data dictionary, and shall not request permission
to write to the health platform.}
{Every health permission the system requests is a read permission, and its data
type appears in the SRS data dictionary as an activity input.}
{Each extra permission is more personal data that could be exposed. One
participant said they look for apps that are frugal with the permissions they
ask for (P01).}
{FMEA H43, L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-7.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-8}{State the purpose of each permission}
{Before requesting each permission, the system shall tell the user which
feature uses that data.}
{For every permission request, a
test user is shown the feature that uses that data before the request appears.}
{Both participants said they are more willing to grant a permission when it is
clear why the app is asking (P01, P02). A user who denies a health permission
earns nothing for that activity and may believe the app is broken.}
{FMEA H1, L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-8. Implemented in part by SRS
Section~6.6, FR\_SetPermissionList.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-9}{Consent and withdrawal}
{The system shall obtain the user's consent before it first reads their health
data, and shall stop reading health data when the user withdraws consent in the
app.}
{A new test user's health data is not read until they consent. After they
withdraw consent in the app, no further health data is read.}
{Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA)
requires consent to collect personal information and lets people withdraw it.
Health data is among the most sensitive data \progname{} handles.}
{L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-9. Implemented in part by SRS
Section~6.6, FR\_SetWithdrawConsent.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-10}{Data minimization}
{The system shall send activity data from the user's mobile device to its
server only as daily totals for each activity type, not as individual activity
records.}
{The activity data a test phone sends to the server contains only daily totals
for each activity type and no individual activity records.}
{Data that never leaves the phone cannot be exposed by a breach of the server. One participant prefers
tools that keep their data on their own device rather than in the cloud (P01).}
{FMEA H43, L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-10.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-11}{No data to third parties}
{The system shall not send users' personal or health data to any third party,
other than a user's email address and password reset link to the email service
that delivers it. Requests to the market data provider shall identify assets
only by symbol.}
{Every outgoing request from a test mobile device and from the server goes to
\progname{}'s own server, the market data provider or the email service.
Requests to the market data provider contain no personal or health data and
identify assets only by symbol. Requests to the email service contain no
personal or health data other than the user's email address and their password
reset link.}
{Participants judge apps by what happens to their data: one chose apps where
their data is not sold (P01), and another wants their data kept secure by a
reputable company (P02). Password reset by email (SRS Section~6.1,
FR\_AcctResetPassword) needs an email service, so it receives only the address
and the reset link.}
{L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-11.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-12}{No location data}
{The system shall not read or store location or route data.}
{The permissions a test build requests include no location permission, and no
location or route data is stored on the phone or the server.}
{None of \progname{}'s features need location. Location or route data, such as
the route maps some workouts store in the health platform, could reveal where a
user lives or regularly goes.}
{FMEA H33, L1 (physical harm), L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-12.}
{Capstone.}

\end{description}

\subsection{Social Feed}

\begin{description}

\hazreq{SEC-13}{Private by default}
{The system shall show a user's statistics only to that user until they choose
to share them, and shall show a shared update only to the friends the user
selected for it. When the user deletes a shared update or removes a friend, the
system shall stop showing the affected shared updates to that friend.}
{A test user's new statistic is visible to no other user. Once shared with
selected friends, it is visible to exactly those friends. After the user
deletes the shared update or removes one of those friends, the shared update
no longer appears in that friend's social feed.}
{Problem Statement Goal 3 measures success by users controlling who sees each
shared statistic. Friends are expected to see only what a user chooses to
share (Problem Statement 1.3).}
{FMEA H30, L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-13. Implemented in part by SRS
Section~6.5, FR\_SocialChooseFriends, FR\_SocialDeleteUpdate and
FR\_SocialRemove.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-14}{No location detail in shared updates}
{The system shall not include location, route or the exact time an activity
took place in shared updates.}
{No shared update shown to a test user's friends contains a location, a route,
or the exact time an activity took place.}
{A route or location, especially combined with when an activity happened, can
reveal where a user lives, works or regularly goes. One participant pointed out
that removing names does not make a person anonymous, because context can
reveal who they are (P01).}
{FMEA H31, H33, L1 (physical harm), L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-14.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SAF-11}{No rankings between users}
{The system shall not rank users against each other by activity, currency or
portfolio performance.}
{No screen shown to a test user orders users by activity, currency or
portfolio performance, or shows a user's position relative to others.}
{Seeing friends' progress can motivate, but comparison becomes harmful when a
user is far behind (P02). A ranking makes that gap explicit and places someone
last. Leaderboards are a stretch goal (Problem Statement Section~3). If they are
built, they should be opt-in.}
{FMEA H9, L2 (psychological harm).}
{SRS Section~6.5, FR\_SocialNoRanking.}
{Capstone. Opt-in leaderboards: Future, with the stretch goal.}

\end{description}

\subsection{Account Security}

\begin{description}

\hazreq{SEC-15}{Authenticated access only}
{The system shall require an authenticated session for every request that reads
or changes a user's data, and shall return another user's data, other than
their username, only when that user has shared it with the requester.}
{A test request without a valid session is rejected. A request for another
user's data, other than their username, that was not shared with the requester
is rejected, and a request for data that was shared succeeds.}
{Health, portfolio and social data must reach only the account it
belongs to or the friends it was shared with. \reqref{SEC-13} sets what a user
shares. Usernames are the exception because friend requests are sent by
username (SRS Section~6.5, FR\_SocialRequest).}
{FMEA H39, L3 (privacy loss), L4 (security loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-15.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-16}{Protect data in transit and at rest}
{The system shall encrypt user data in transit between the phone and the
server, and when stored on the server, including in backups.}
{Inspecting the network traffic between a test phone and the server, the data
stored on the server, and its backups shows no readable user data.}
{Data in transit can be intercepted on public networks, and stored data can be
exposed if the server or a backup is compromised. Participants want their data
kept secure (P02).}
{FMEA H40, H43, L3 (privacy loss), L4 (security loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-16.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-17}{Session expiry}
{The system shall end a session when the user signs out of it or when it has
not been used for \texttt{SESSION\_TIMEOUT}, after which the user must sign in
again.}
{A test session is rejected after the user signs out of it, and after it is
left unused for longer than \texttt{SESSION\_TIMEOUT}. In both cases the user
must sign in again to continue.}
{A lost or shared phone, or a stolen session, gives access to a user's health
and portfolio data for as long as the session stays valid. Ending
unused sessions limits that window, and ending a session at sign-out stops a
copied session from being reused (SRS Section~6.1, FR\_AcctSignOut).}
{FMEA H36, L3 (privacy loss), L4 (security loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-17.}
{Capstone.}

\hazreq{SEC-18}{In-app account deletion}
{The system shall let the user delete their account from within the app, and
shall delete their account details, profile, activity totals, currency
balance, holdings, trades, learning progress, shared updates, friend list and
friend requests from the server and its backups within
\texttt{DATA\_DELETION\_PERIOD} of the request.}
{After a test user deletes their account in the app, they can no longer sign
in. Within \texttt{DATA\_DELETION\_PERIOD}, neither the server nor its backups
hold any of their account details, profile, activity totals, currency balance,
holdings, trades, learning progress, shared updates, friend list or friend
requests, and none of their shared updates appear in friends' social feeds.}
{PIPEDA lets people withdraw consent and limits keeping personal information
longer than it is needed. The Apple App Store also requires apps that let users
create an account to offer account deletion within the app (App Store Review
Guideline 5.1.1(v)).}
{FMEA H38, L3 (privacy loss).}
{SRS Section~7.3, SEC-18. Implemented in part by SRS
Section~6.1, FR\_AcctDelete.}
{Capstone.}

\end{description}
