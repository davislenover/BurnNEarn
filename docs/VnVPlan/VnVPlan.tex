\section{General Information}

This section identifies the software under test, states the objectives of
verification and validation (V\&V) and what is out of scope, summarizes the
custom documents, and lists the documents this plan draws on.

\subsection{Summary}

% \wss{Say what software is being tested.  Give its name and a brief overview of
%   its general functions.}

The software being tested is \progname{}, a mobile application in which
young adults earn virtual currency from the physical activity their phone
records and invest it in a simulated market that follows real-world prices.
\progname{} consists of a mobile app for Android and iOS, built with React
Native and TypeScript, and a server built with FastAPI and a PostgreSQL
database. The app reads activity from the phone's health platform (Apple
HealthKit or Google Health Connect), and the server obtains asset prices from
the market data provider, Finnhub. The full system and its context are
described in Section~2.3 of the Software Requirements
Specification~\citep[SRS;][]{SRS0}.

The functions of \progname{} fall into the six feature areas of SRS
Section~6, which also organize the system tests in Section~\ref{SecSystemTests}
of this plan:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Accounts and profile:} creating an account, signing in and out,
        resetting a password, editing the profile, downloading a copy of one's
        data, and deleting the account.
  \item \textbf{Activity and rewards:} reading activity records for the
        supported activity types, converting plausible activity into virtual
        currency up to a daily cap, and tracking goals, streaks, rest days and
        milestones.
  \item \textbf{Simulated market and portfolio:} buying and selling whole shares
        of at least 50 real-world stocks at prices that follow the real market,
        showing holdings and portfolio value, and practising in market scenarios
        that replay past periods.
  \item \textbf{Learning and feedback:} explaining financial and fitness
        concepts where they appear, giving feedback on the user's own trades and
        portfolio changes, and testing understanding with knowledge checks.
  \item \textbf{Social feed:} adding friends by username and sharing selected
        statistics and milestones with chosen friends.
  \item \textbf{Reminders and settings:} sending reminders only in the periods
        the user chooses, and letting the user review and withdraw health data
        permissions.
\end{itemize}

Because \progname{} handles health data and presents financial information
that users may act on, the plan also verifies the safety and security
requirements identified in the Hazard Analysis~\citep[HA;][]{HA}, which the
SRS restates in Sections~6 and~7.3.

\subsection{Objectives}

The objectives below are the qualities the team most needs to be confident in,
given the time and people available. They are listed in priority order, with
the SRS goals (G) and non-functional requirements (NFR) each one comes from.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Correct currency, rewards, and portfolio values.} Build
    confidence that activity earns the right amount of virtual currency, that
    each activity is rewarded only once, and that balances, trades, and
    portfolio values are calculated correctly, including when a trade is
    interrupted or repeated (G4, G5, NFR1 Accuracy, NFR6 Reliability). This
    comes first because users judge the app by whether its numbers are right,
    and in an educational app a wrong number also teaches a wrong lesson.
  \item \textbf{Private and secure user data.} Demonstrate that private
    information, including health data, is available only to authenticated and
    authorized users, and that nothing is shared with friends unless the user
    chooses to share it (G3, NFR7 Security). \progname{} handles health data,
    so a leak would harm users and break the privacy rules it must follow
    (SRS, SC6).
  \item \textbf{Adequate usability and accessibility.} Validate through
    usability testing that first-time users can complete the core activity and
    investing tasks without help, including with enlarged text, and that
    participants improve on the before-and-after knowledge check (G1, G2, NFR2
    Usability, NFR8 Accessibility). The results will be reported in the
    Usability Report.
  \item \textbf{Core features on Android and iOS.} Demonstrate that the core
    features work on both supported platforms (NFR4 Portability). Since one
    React Native codebase targets both, the same acceptance tests are run on
    each.
\end{itemize}

The following are out of scope, because the team does not control them or does
not have the resources to verify them:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{External libraries and services.} The phone's health platform
    (Apple HealthKit or Health Connect), the libraries that connect to it, the
    market data provider, and frameworks such as FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, and
    PostgreSQL are assumed to have been verified by their own teams. How
    \progname{} handles wrong, missing, late, or duplicated data from these
    services is still in scope (Hazard Analysis, CA4).
  \item \textbf{Accuracy of recorded activity and market prices.}
    \progname{} rewards the activity the health platform records and values
    portfolios at the provider's prices. Whether a phone counts steps
    correctly, or whether a quoted price matches the exchange, cannot be
    checked by the team. The team verifies how \progname{} uses these values,
    but not the values themselves.
  \item \textbf{Load and scalability testing.} No requirement covers large
    numbers of users, and during the project the app will be used only by the
    team, the supervisor, and usability test participants. Performance is
    checked only as stated in NFR5.
  \item \textbf{Attacks from a compromised phone or hosting provider.}
    Security testing covers \progname{}'s own authentication and
    authorization. A rooted or jailbroken phone and the hosting provider's
    physical security are outside the team's control (Hazard Analysis, CA3 and
    CA5). No external penetration test is planned.
  \item \textbf{Long-term effects on users.} Whether \progname{} changes users'
    fitness habits or financial decisions over months cannot be measured within
    the project. Learning is measured only by the short-term knowledge check in
    G1.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implementation Verification}

The team will verify FitFolio's implementation through dynamic testing, code inspection, and automated static analysis. Verification will check the implementation against the Software Requirements Specification (SRS), including the safety and security requirements identified in the Hazard Analysis. These activities will follow the review and merge process established in the Development Plan's Workflow Plan and Definition of Done.

\subsection{Automated Testing and Verification Tools}

Ruff lints and formats the backend's Python code, and pytest runs its unit and
integration tests. For the frontend, ESLint and Prettier lint and format the
TypeScript code, and Jest runs its unit tests. gitleaks scans the whole
repository for secrets. Each runs in GitHub Actions on every pull request and
every push to \texttt{main}, and the team's workflow requires every check to
pass before a pull request is merged (Development Plan, Workflow Plan). The
reasons for choosing each tool are given in the Development Plan's Expected
Technology section.

\subsubsection{Linting and Formatting}

Ruff is used to lint and format the backend's Python code, in place of the
separate Black and Flake8 tools. It is configured in
\path{src/backend/pyproject.toml} and enforces the team's coding standard
(Development Plan, Coding Standard), including the PEP 8 line length of 79
characters. The enabled rule sets are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{E, W:} pycodestyle errors and warnings (PEP 8 style).
  \item \textbf{F:} pyflakes, such as unused imports and undefined names.
  \item \textbf{I:} import order.
  \item \textbf{B:} flake8-bugbear, which flags likely bugs such as mutable
    default arguments.
  \item \textbf{UP:} pyupgrade, which flags syntax that has a newer
    equivalent.
  \item \textbf{N:} PEP 8 naming conventions.
  \item \textbf{SIM:} flake8-simplify, which flags code that can be written
    more simply.
\end{itemize}

The \texttt{Lint (Ruff)} job in \path{.github/workflows/backend-ci.yml} runs
\texttt{ruff check} and \texttt{ruff format --check} on both the backend source
and its tests in \path{test/backend}. The job fails if any rule is broken or
any file is not formatted, so unformatted code cannot reach \texttt{main}.

The frontend's TypeScript code will be linted with ESLint, which catches
common mistakes, and formatted with Prettier, configured to use four spaces
for indentation to match the coding standard. Both will run in CI on every
pull request and fail the check if any rule is broken or any file is not
formatted, in the same way as Ruff.

\subsubsection{Unit and Integration Testing}

pytest is used for the backend's automated tests, which live in
\path{test/backend}. The \texttt{Test (pytest + PostgreSQL)} job in
\path{.github/workflows/backend-ci.yml} starts a PostgreSQL 18 container,
applies the database migrations with Alembic, and then runs the full test
suite. Tests that read or write data therefore run against a real database
with the same schema the app uses, rather than a mock. The CI database is
created fresh for each run and holds no real user data.

Code coverage is measured with pytest-cov. Each CI run prints a coverage
report that lists, for every module in the backend's \texttt{app} package, the
percentage of lines executed and the line numbers that were missed. Reviewers
can use this report to check that a pull request tests the code it adds. No
minimum coverage is enforced at this stage. Coverage results will be
summarized in the VnV Report.

The frontend's unit tests will use Jest, which works directly with React
Native, and will run in CI alongside the backend tests. Jest has built-in
coverage reporting, so frontend coverage will be reported on each run and
summarized in the VnV Report in the same way.

\subsubsection{Secret Scanning}

The repository is public, so an API key, password, or token is exposed as soon
as it is pushed. Secrets are kept in a local \path{.env} file, which Git
ignores, and gitleaks checks for any that slip into a commit at two points:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Before each commit:} a pre-commit hook, configured in
    \path{.pre-commit-config.yaml}, scans the staged changes and stops the
    commit if they contain a secret. Each member sets it up once per clone, as
    described in \path{CONTRIBUTING.md}.
  \item \textbf{In CI:} the \texttt{Secret scan} workflow in
    \path{.github/workflows/secret-scan.yml} scans the commits in every pull
    request and every push to \texttt{main}. This catches a secret even if the
    hook was skipped or never set up.
\end{itemize}

Both checks use gitleaks 8.30.1, so they apply the same detection rules. Any
secret found is redacted in the output, so a CI run does not expose it again.
A secret that reaches the repository is revoked and replaced, since removing
it from the history does not make it private again.

\subsection{Tests for Functional Requirements}

The tests in this section are grouped into the same six areas as the
functional requirements (FRs) in SRS Section 6: Accounts and Profile, Activity
and Rewards, Simulated Market and Portfolio, Learning and Feedback, Social
Feed, and Reminders and Settings. Each test is derived from the fit criterion
of the FRs it covers, so an FR is verified when all of its tests pass. Every
FR is covered by at least one test, and the traceability table that ends the
System Tests section maps each test back to its FRs.

Each test follows the format of the template, with these conventions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Test ID:} \texttt{test-}\textit{area}\texttt{-}\textit{n},
    where \textit{area} is \texttt{acct}, \texttt{act}, \texttt{mkt},
    \texttt{learn}, \texttt{social}, or \texttt{remind}, matching the SRS
    labels for each area, and \textit{n} numbers the tests within the area.
  \item \textbf{Requirements:} the FRs the test covers, by their SRS number.
  \item \textbf{Control:} \textit{Automatic} tests run with pytest against the
    backend's API, or with Jest for the frontend, on every pull request (see
    Automated Testing and Verification Tools). \textit{Manual} tests are run by
    a tester on an Android or iOS test device, mostly to check what the user
    sees.
\end{itemize}

Automated tests send activity records and market prices to the backend
directly, in place of the health platform and the market data provider, so
every input and expected value is known. The accuracy of those services is out
of scope (see Objectives). Manual tests record activity on a test device
through the phone's health platform, so they also check that \progname{} reads
it correctly.

Tests that depend on the calendar day run in a fixed time zone, with the
backend's clock set to known dates, so that daily totals, streaks, and rest
days can be tested without waiting for real days to pass. Expected values use
the symbolic constants in SRS Appendix C, such as
\texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_CURRENCY}, with their current values given in parentheses.
If a constant changes, the expected outputs of the tests that use it change
with it.

\subsubsection{Accounts and Profile}

These tests cover the accounts and profile requirements in SRS Section 6.1
(FR1 to FR11). Automatic tests create their own test accounts through the
backend's API, with email addresses in the reserved \texttt{.test} domain, so
no message can reach a real person. Tests of password reset replace the email
service with a test mailbox that records every message sent to it, so the
test can read the reset link and check who it was sent to. Passwords in the
inputs are at least \texttt{MIN\_PASSWORD\_LENGTH} (8) characters long, so
that the password rule in the Hazard Analysis (SEC-38) does not reject them
for a reason unrelated to the test.

\begin{enumerate}

\item{\textbf{test-acct-1: Creating an account and signing in}\\}

Requirements: FR1, FR4

Control: Automatic

Initial State: No account uses the email address \texttt{sam@fitfolio.test}
or the username \texttt{sam\_test}.

Input: A sign-up request with the email address \texttt{sam@fitfolio.test},
the username \texttt{sam\_test}, the password \texttt{Walk2026run}, and the age
confirmed. Then two sign-in requests with that email address: one with the
password \texttt{Walk2026run}, and one with \texttt{Walk2026ruN}.

Output: The sign-up succeeds and an account with that email address exists.
The first sign-in succeeds and returns a session. The second is refused and
returns no session.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR1 is that a user who enters an
unused email address and a valid password has an account and can sign in with
them, which the first sign-in shows. FR4 signs in a user with the correct
password and refuses a wrong one. The wrong password differs from the correct
one only in the case of its last letter, so a comparison that ignored case
would let it through.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends the three requests to the
backend's API and checks each response.

\item{\textbf{test-acct-2: No account without confirming age}\\}

Requirements: FR2

Control: Automatic and Manual

Initial State: The email addresses \texttt{young1@fitfolio.test} and
\texttt{young2@fitfolio.test} are not used by any account.

Input: Two sign-up requests to the backend, with otherwise valid details:
\begin{itemize}
  \item for \texttt{young1@fitfolio.test}, with the age confirmation set to
    false;
  \item for \texttt{young2@fitfolio.test}, with the age confirmation left out
    of the request.
\end{itemize}
Then, on a test device, the tester fills in the sign-up screen for
\texttt{young3@fitfolio.test} without confirming their age.

Output: Both requests are refused, and signing in with either email address
fails because no account exists. On the device, no account is created for
\texttt{young3@fitfolio.test}, and the screen shows that the age
confirmation is required.

Test Case Derivation: FR2 (Hazard Analysis, SEC-37) creates no account until
the user confirms they are at least \texttt{MIN\_USER\_AGE} (18) years old.
Each case uses its own email address, so that an account existing afterwards
shows which case failed. The two requests check the backend: a confirmation
set to false must be refused, and so must a request with no confirmation at
all, since a backend that only checked for false would treat a missing value
as confirmed. The backend is tested directly because a modified app could
skip the check on the sign-up screen (Hazard Analysis, H43). The device case
checks that the sign-up screen enforces the confirmation too and tells the
user why. It needs only one case, because the screen always sends a value.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends the two requests and then
tries to sign in with each address. The tester then tries the sign-up screen
on a test device and records whether an account was created.

\item{\textbf{test-acct-3: Unique usernames}\\}

Requirements: FR3

Control: Automatic

Initial State: An account with the username \texttt{sam\_test} exists, as
created in test-acct-1. No account uses the email address
\texttt{jordan@fitfolio.test}.

Input: A sign-up request for \texttt{jordan@fitfolio.test} with the username
\texttt{sam\_test}, followed by a second request with the same details except
the username \texttt{jordan\_test}.

Output: The first request is refused with a message asking the user to choose
a different username, and no account is created. The second request
succeeds.

Test Case Derivation: FR3 asks a user who chooses a username already in use
to choose a different one. The second request shows that the refusal was
caused by the username alone, since every other detail is the same.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends both requests and checks
each response and which accounts exist afterwards.

\item{\textbf{test-acct-4: Signing out}\\}

Requirements: FR5

Control: Manual

Initial State: A test user with a balance of 250 and the username
\texttt{sam\_test}, signed in on a test device.

Input: The tester opens the home screen and notes the balance shown, signs
out, closes \progname{} completely, and opens it again.

Output: After reopening, \progname{} shows the sign-in screen. Neither the
balance of 250 nor the username \texttt{sam\_test} appears on any screen
reachable without signing in.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR5 is that after signing out,
opening the app shows the sign-in screen and none of the user's data. Closing
the app completely checks that the sign-out is kept on the phone and is not
just a change of screen. The balance and username are known values, so the
tester can check that neither is shown.

How test will be performed: The tester follows the input steps on the test
device and records each screen shown after reopening.

\item{\textbf{test-acct-5: Resetting a forgotten password}\\}

Requirements: FR6

Control: Automatic

Initial State: An account for \texttt{sam@fitfolio.test} with the password
\texttt{Walk2026run}, and a second account for \texttt{jordan@fitfolio.test}.
The email service is replaced by a test mailbox.

Input: A password reset request for \texttt{sam@fitfolio.test}. The test opens
the link in the email the mailbox receives and sets the new password
\texttt{Swim2027lap}. It then signs in with each password.

Output: The mailbox receives exactly one email, addressed to
\texttt{sam@fitfolio.test} and containing a reset link. After the reset,
signing in with \texttt{Walk2026run} is refused and signing in with
\texttt{Swim2027lap} succeeds. The account for \texttt{jordan@fitfolio.test}
receives no email and its password is unchanged.

Test Case Derivation: FR6 (Hazard Analysis, SEC-40) sends the link to the
account's own email address, and its fit criterion is that the user can then
sign in only with the new password. Checking the old password catches a reset
that adds a new password without removing the old one. The second account
checks that a reset for one account does not reach or change another.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends the reset request, reads the
email from the test mailbox, follows the link through the API, and sends the
sign-in requests.

\item{\textbf{test-acct-6: Editing the profile}\\}

Requirements: FR7

Control: Manual

Initial State: A test user signed in on a test device, with a height of 170
cm in their profile.

Input: The tester changes the height to 175 cm and saves it, closes
\progname{} completely, opens it again, and opens the profile.

Output: The profile shows a height of 175 cm.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR7 is that the user sees the new
value the next time they open their profile. Closing the app between saving
and reopening checks that the change was stored, and not only kept on the
screen.

How test will be performed: The tester follows the input steps on the test
device and records the height shown.

\item{\textbf{test-acct-7: Using every feature without height or weight}\\}

Requirements: FR8

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user with no height or weight in their profile, a
balance of 0, the health notice and simulated market notice acknowledged, and
one accepted friend. Test asset A is priced at 20.00, its market is open, and
its price is within \texttt{QUOTE\_STALENESS\_LIMIT} (15 minutes).

Input: A daily total of 5,000 steps for day $d$. The user then buys 1 share of

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR8 names four features, earning,
trading, learning and sharing, and the test uses each one with both values
empty. 5,000 steps earn $\lfloor 5000 / 100 \rfloor = 50$ (SRS Section 6.7),

weight would refuse its request or fail to calculate a result.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends each request through the API
and checks each response, the balance, and the friend's feed.

\subsubsection{Activity and Rewards}

These tests cover the activity and rewards requirements in SRS Section 6.2
(FR12 to FR27). Expected currency comes from the daily reward $R_d$ in SRS
Section 6.7, with the values in SRS Appendix C: one unit of currency per 100
steps or wheelchair pushes, one per minute of cycling, swimming, or another
workout, and at most \texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_CURRENCY} (300) per day.

The server calculates rewards only from the daily activity totals the app
sends (Hazard Analysis, SEC-24), and stores no start or end times (SRS Data
Dictionary, Activity Record). Tests of the reward rules therefore run against
the backend with daily totals. Tests that need the time of each activity,
such as assigning it to a calendar day or checking a workout's length, run
against the frontend with Jest. The plausibility limits are input constraints
on activity records, so each limit is tested at its value and just above it.

\begin{enumerate}

\item{\textbf{test-act-1: Earning for each activity type}\\}

Requirements: FR12, FR13

Control: Automatic

Initial State: Six test users, each with a balance of 0, the health notice
acknowledged, no activity goal, and no activity on day $d$.

Input: For day $d$, users 1 to 5 each have a total for one supported type:
2,550 steps, 1,099 wheelchair pushes, 20 minutes of cycling, 15 minutes of
swimming, and 30 minutes of strength training, which counts as another
workout. User 6 has all five totals.

Output: The balances of users 1 to 5 increase by 25, 10, 20, 15, and 30. The
balance of user 6 increases by 100.

Test Case Derivation: Users 1 to 5 each have a single type, so each balance
increase is that type's reward alone, and a type that earns nothing or earns
at the wrong rate shows up on its own (FR12). By $R_d$, each type earns
$\lfloor x_{t,d} / u_t \rfloor$, so 2,550 steps earn 25 and 1,099 pushes earn
10, which also checks that partial units are rounded down. Each minute of a
workout earns 1. User 6 checks that $R_d$ adds up the rewards of all types
(FR13): $25 + 10 + 20 + 15 + 30 = 100$. This is below the daily cap of 300, so
the cap cannot hide a wrong sum.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends each user's daily totals to
the backend, then reads each balance through the API.

\item{\textbf{test-act-2: Daily earning cap}\\}

Requirements: FR18

Control: Automatic

Initial State: Three test users, each with a balance of 0, the health notice
acknowledged, and no activity on day $d$.

Input: For day $d$, the users have totals of 29,900, 30,000, and 40,000 steps.

Output: The balances increase by 299, 300, and 300.

Test Case Derivation: $R_d = \min(300, \lfloor x_{t,d} / 100 \rfloor)$ gives
299, 300, and $\min(300, 400) = 300$. The three totals check the cap just
below, at, and above \texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_CURRENCY} (300).

How test will be performed: As in test-act-1.

\item{\textbf{test-act-3: Activity counts toward the day in the user's time
zone}\\}

Requirements: FR18

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user whose phone is set to the America/Toronto time zone
(UTC$-$5 in January).

Input: Two step records: 20,000 steps ending at 23:30 local time on January
14, 2027 (04:30 UTC on January 15), and 20,000 steps ending at 00:30 local
time on January 15, 2027.

Output: The app's daily totals are 20,000 steps for January 14 and 20,000
steps for January 15.

Test Case Derivation: FR18 counts each activity toward the calendar day in the
user's time zone at the time of the activity. Each day then earns
$\lfloor 20000 / 100 \rfloor = 200$. Grouping the records by UTC day instead
would put 40,000 steps on January 15, which earns only the cap of 300, so the
test detects the wrong grouping.

How test will be performed: A Jest test passes the two records, with their
timestamps and the user's time zone, to the app's code that builds daily
totals, and checks the totals it returns.

\item{\textbf{test-act-4: Day with no activity data}\\}

\item{\textbf{test-act-5: Daily step and push limits}\\}

Requirements: FR17

Control: Automatic

Initial State: Four test users, each with a balance of 0, the health notice
acknowledged, no activity goal, a streak of 6, and no activity on day $d$.

Input: For day $d$, the users have totals of 150,000 steps, 150,001 steps,
100,000 wheelchair pushes, and 100,001 wheelchair pushes.

Output: The totals at each limit (150,000 steps and 100,000 pushes) are
counted: those users' balances increase by 300, the totals appear in their
activity trends, their streaks become 7, and they reach the 7-day streak
milestone. The totals over each limit earn nothing, do not appear in the
users' trends, reset their streaks to 0, reach no milestone, and are marked as
not counted in the API's response.

Test Case Derivation: Each user has one total, so each limit is checked on its
own. The limits are \texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_\allowbreak STEPS} (150,000) and
\texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_\allowbreak PUSHES} (100,000), and FR17 leaves out a record
only when it exceeds its limit, so a total at the limit is counted and a total
one above it is not. Testing both sides of each limit catches a check that
uses $\geq$ instead of $>$. The totals at the limit earn
more than the cap, so $R_d = 300$. With no goal set, any counted activity
extends the streak (SRS Section 6.7), so a streak of 6 becomes 7. FR25 shows
each milestone in \texttt{MILESTONES} (streaks of 7, 30, and 100 days, and
1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 currency earned in total), so a streak of 7 reaches
the first streak milestone. A total over the limit is left out of
$x_{t,d}$, so $R_d = 0$ and day $d$ does not count toward the streak, which
resets it to 0. Starting from a streak of 6 means that a wrongly counted total
would reach the 7-day milestone, so the test also checks that a total that is
not counted reaches no milestone.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends each user's totals, then reads
the balance, trends, streak, and each total's status through the API.

\item{\textbf{test-act-6: Workout length limit}\\}

Output: The app counts the first workout, giving a cycling total of 720

Test Case Derivation: \texttt{MAX\_WORKOUT\_DURATION} is 12 hours, and FR17
leaves out only a workout longer than it, so the two records check the limit
at its value and one minute above it. They are on separate days because two

this limit before it sends the daily total.

How test will be performed: A Jest test passes the records to the app's code
that builds daily totals, and checks the total and each record's status.

\item{\textbf{test-act-8: No currency before the health notice is
acknowledged}\\}

Requirements: FR14

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A new test user with a balance of 0 and no activity goal, who
has not acknowledged the health notice or consented to \progname{} reading
their health data.

Input: The backend receives a total of 5,000 steps for day $d$. On day $d+1$,
the user records 3,000 steps, then acknowledges the notice and consents. The
backend then receives the totals for days $d$ and $d+1$.

Output: The balance is 0 at the end of day $d$ and 30 at the end of day $d+1$.
At the end of day $d+1$, the streak is 1 and the activity trends show only day
$d+1$. The app reads no health data and asks for no consent until the notice is
acknowledged, and its first sync sends no total for a day before the user's
first day.

Test Case Derivation: FR14 allows no currency until the notice is
acknowledged, even if a daily total reaches the server, so day $d$ earns
nothing at first. The app reads no health data before then, so that total can
only come from an app bug or a modified app, and the test checks that the
server does not rely on the app to enforce the notice. The user's first day is
the calendar day they acknowledge the notice, $d+1$ (SRS Section 6.7), and
SEC-20 does not reward records from before it, so day $d$ still earns nothing
when its total is sent again. The app should not send that total, so this also
checks that the server does not rely on the app to leave it out. Day $d+1$ earns
$\lfloor 3000 / 100 \rfloor = 30$. Its steps come before the acknowledgement,
which checks that the cut-off is the calendar day and not the moment of
acknowledgement. The two days have different totals so that the final balance
shows which days were rewarded: 80 would mean day $d$ was rewarded too. With no
goal set, the streak would be 2 if day $d$ counted (SRS Section 6.7).

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends day $d$'s total, moves the
backend's clock to day $d+1$, sends the acknowledgement, the consent, and then
both days' totals, and reads the balance at the end of each day, then the
streak and trends. A Jest test replaces the health platform with a test version
that records every read, as in test-remind-7, and holds records from the
previous day. It starts the app for a new user, checks that no read and no
consent request happen before the notice is acknowledged, and checks that the
first sync sends no total for the previous day.

\item{\textbf{test-act-9: Health notice shown before the consent request}\\}

Test Case Derivation: This is the notice and the order FR14 requires. A new

How test will be performed: The tester compares the notice's text with FR14,
checks that no consent request appears before acknowledging it, and checks the

\item{\textbf{test-act-11: Rewards ignore body weight and calories}\\}

Requirements: FR26

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user with a body weight of 70 kg, a balance of 0, the
health notice acknowledged, and no activity on days $d$ to $d+2$.

Input: The user has a total of 30 minutes of cycling on each of days $d$,
$d+1$, and $d+2$. Before day $d+1$, the weight is changed to 90 kg, and before
day $d+2$, it is removed.

Output: The balance increases by 30 on each day, to 90 at the end of day
$d+2$.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR26 changes and removes the
user's weight, so this test does the same, with the same activity on each day.
Only the weight differs between the days, so if it affected the reward, at
least one day would earn a different amount. $R_d$ depends only on activity
totals and earning rates, so 30 minutes of cycling earns 30 on each day. The
activity record has no calorie field (SRS Data Dictionary), so calories cannot
reach the reward calculation. Goals are pairs of an activity type and an
amount (SRS Section 6.7), and \texttt{MILESTONES} are streaks of 7, 30, and
100 days and 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 currency earned in total (SRS Appendix
C), so neither uses weight or calories either.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends each day's total and the
weight changes, moving the backend's clock between days, and reads the balance
at the end of each day.

\item{\textbf{test-act-12: Goal changes apply from the next day}\\}

Requirements: FR19

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user with a daily goal of 10,000 steps, a streak of 3,
the health notice acknowledged, and no activity on days $d$ and $d+1$.

Input: On day $d$, the user lowers the goal to 5,000 steps. The user then has
a total of 6,000 steps on each of days $d$ and $d+1$.

Output: The goal in effect is 10,000 steps on day $d$ and 5,000 steps on day
$d+1$. The streak is 0 at the end of day $d$ and 1 at the end of day $d+1$.

Test Case Derivation: FR19 applies a goal change from the next day, so the
goal in effect on day $d$ is still 10,000. Day $d$'s 6,000 steps miss it, so
day $d$ does not count toward the streak (SRS Section 6.7) and the streak
resets to 0. On day $d+1$, 6,000 steps meet the new goal of 5,000, so the
streak becomes 1. The daily total is between the old and new goals, so
whether each day counts shows which goal was in effect. The goal is lowered,
not raised, because applying changes from the next day is what stops a user
from lowering a goal to keep the current day's streak alive (Hazard Analysis,
SAF-4). The streak starts at 3 so that a change applied on the same day would
give a streak of 4 instead of 0.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends the goal change and then the
day's total, moves the backend's clock to day $d+1$, sends that day's total,
and reads the goal in effect and the streak for each day through the API.

\item{\textbf{test-act-13: Earning without a goal}\\}

Requirements: FR20

Control: Automatic

Initial State: Two test users, each with a balance of 0, the health notice

Input: For day $d$, both users have a total of 4,000 steps.

Test Case Derivation: User 1 is the case in the fit criterion of FR20: a user

\item{\textbf{test-act-14: No automatic goal changes}\\}

\item{\textbf{test-act-15: Streak with a goal}\\}

Requirements: FR22, FR23

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user with a daily goal of 5,000 steps, a streak of 0,
the health notice acknowledged, no rest days, and no activity on days $d$ to
$d+4$.

Input: The user has totals of 6,000, 5,000, and 6,000 steps on days $d$ to
$d+2$. Day $d+3$ is marked as a rest day and has no activity. Day $d+4$ is not
a rest day and has 4,999 steps.

Output: The streak at the end of days $d$ to $d+4$ is 1, 2, 3, 4, and 0.

Test Case Derivation: The days follow the fit criterion of FR22: three days
that meet the goal give a streak of 3, a rest day then gives 4, and a missed
day then gives 0. By SRS Section 6.7, a day counts toward the streak when the
user meets every goal, so $x_{t,d} \geq g$, or when it is a rest day. Day
$d+1$ has exactly the goal and day $d+4$ is one step below it, so the goal is
checked at its value and just below, which catches a check that uses $>$
instead of $\geq$. Day $d+4$ also has activity, which shows that with a goal
set, recording activity is not enough to keep the streak. As in the fit
criterion of FR23, the rest day has no activity, so the streak continues
because of the rest day alone.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends each day's total or rest day,
moving the backend's clock between days, and reads the streak at the end of
each day through the API.

\item{\textbf{test-act-16: Streak with more than one goal}\\}

\item{\textbf{test-act-17: Streak without a goal}\\}

\item{\textbf{test-act-18: Rest day limit}\\}

Requirements: FR23

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user with no rest days marked, in the week from Monday,
January 4, 2027, to Sunday, January 10, 2027.

Input: The user asks to mark rest days on Tuesday, January 5, Thursday, January
7, Sunday, January 10, and Monday, January 11, 2027. Each request is made on
the day it is for.

Output: The requests for January 5, 7, and 11 are accepted, and the request
for January 10 is refused. The user's rest days are January 5, 7, and 11.

Test Case Derivation: \texttt{REST\_DAYS\_PER\_PERIOD} is 2 and
\texttt{STREAK\_PERIOD} is a calendar week from Monday to Sunday in the user's
time zone (SRS Appendix C). January 5 and 7 use the two rest days of the week
of January 4, so the request for January 10 is a third in the same week and
is refused. January 10 is a Sunday, so the request also checks that Sunday
ends the week that began on Monday. If weeks started on Sunday, it would be
the first rest day of a new week and would be accepted. January 11 is the next
Monday, so it starts a new week and is accepted. A rolling 7-day window would
still contain January 5 and 7 and would refuse it. Each request is made on its
own day so that the test does not depend on whether rest days can be marked
for other days.

How test will be performed: A pytest test moves the backend's clock to each
date, sends the rest day request, checks whether it is accepted, and then
reads the user's rest days through the API.

\item{\textbf{test-act-19: No penalty for a missed goal}\\}

\subsubsection{Simulated Market and Portfolio}

These tests cover the simulated market and portfolio requirements in SRS
Section 6.3 (FR28 to FR49). Expected balances, holdings, and portfolio values
come from the definitions of trades and portfolio value in SRS Section 6.7,
and are compared to within \texttt{VALUE\_TOLERANCE} (0.01) (NFR1). All
prices are in \texttt{QUOTE\_CURRENCY} (US dollars).

Automated tests replace the market data provider, Finnhub (SRS Appendix C),
with a test provider that returns the prices each test chooses, so every
expected price is known. Each test also sets whether the asset's market is
open, so tests that depend on market hours can run at any time of day. Price
age is measured with the backend's clock. Manual tests use the real provider,
so they also check that \progname{} shows real prices. The exceptions are
manual tests that need a price change the real market is unlikely to give
during the test, such as a large change in portfolio value. These run the
release build against a test backend that uses the test provider.

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-2: Prices in one currency}\\}

Without it, the test could not fail. Each share costs 50.00, well below the

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-3: Adding and removing an asset}\\}

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-6: Price time and the delayed marker}\\}

Requirements: FR33

Control: Automatic

Initial State: An asset in the live market with one price, obtained from the
test provider at time $p$. The provider sends no later price during the test.

Input: While the asset's market is open, the price is read at 15 minutes
after $p$ and at 15 minutes and 1 second after $p$. The market then closes,
and the price is read 2 days after $p$.

Output: All three reads show the price with time $p$. Only the read at 15
minutes and 1 second after $p$ is marked as delayed.

Test Case Derivation: \texttt{QUOTE\_STALENESS\_LIMIT} is 15 minutes (SRS
Appendix C), from Problem Statement Goal 4. FR33 marks a price as delayed only
when it is older than the limit, so a price exactly 15 minutes old is not
delayed and one a second older is. The backend's clock is set by the test, so
a difference of one second can be tested exactly. The read while the market is
closed is far older than the limit, which checks that the marker depends on
the market being open, as the fit criterion of FR33 requires. This is the case
of a price from the last close being shown overnight or on a weekend. No new
price is obtained during the test, so every read returns the stored price.
Reading a stored price does not change the time it was obtained (SRS Data
Dictionary, Market Data), so all three reads show time $p$. A time that
changed on each read would hide the delay.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sets the test provider's price,
moves the backend's clock to each time, and reads the price, its time, and
whether it is delayed through the API. A Jest test then renders the app's
price display with each of the three responses and checks that it shows the
time and shows the ``Delayed'' marker only for the delayed price.

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-9: Buying whole shares}\\}

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-11: Trades limited by balance and holdings}\\}

Requirements: FR37

Control: Automatic

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-12: Interrupted trades}\\}

Requirements: FR37

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user with a balance of 1,000 who holds no assets and has
acknowledged the simulated market notice. An asset in the live market with a
current price of 40.00.

Input: While the market is open, the user asks to buy 1 share, and the
backend is made to fail after it updates one of the balance and the holding
but before it updates the other. The user then makes a new request to buy 1
share, with no failure. Last, the user asks to sell 1 share, and the backend
is made to fail in the same way.

Output: After the first purchase, the balance is 1,000 and the user holds no

share received, or the reverse. The order of the two updates depends on the
backend's design, so the failure comes after whichever update is first. The
completed purchase is the second case in the fit criterion of FR37, a trade
that changes both values: $1000 - 40 = 960$ and one share. Purchases and
sales update the values in opposite directions, so both are interrupted.

How test will be performed: A pytest test replaces the backend's step for the
later of the two updates with one that raises an error, sends the trade, and
then reads the balance and holdings through the API. It restores the step
before the second purchase and replaces it again before the sale.

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-13: Duplicate trade requests}\\}

Requirements: FR38

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-15: Trades queued while the market is closed}\\}

Requirements: FR40

queued with the notice.

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-16: Queued trades that can no longer be covered}\\}

Requirements: FR40

Control: Automatic

Initial State: An asset whose market is closed, with a last price of 40.00.
Three test users who have acknowledged the simulated market notice. Users 1
and 2 hold no assets and have balances of 100.00 and 99.99. User 3 holds 1
share of the asset and has a balance of 0.

Input: While the market is closed, users 1 and 2 each ask to buy 2 shares,
and user 3 makes two separate requests to sell 1 share. The market then opens,
and the first price is 50.00.

Output: User 1's purchase is executed, leaving a balance of 0.00 and 2 shares.
User 2's purchase is rejected, user 2 is told, and their balance is still
99.99 with no shares. One of user 3's sales is executed, leaving a balance of
50.00 and no shares. The other is not executed, and user 3 is told.

Test Case Derivation: When they are requested, all the trades can be covered
at the last price: 2 shares cost $2 \cdot 40 = 80$, which both balances cover,
and user 3 holds the share that each sale needs. At the opening price, 2
shares cost $2 \cdot 50 = 100$, which equals user 1's balance and is 0.01 more
than user 2's, so the limit is tested at its value and just past it, as in
test-mkt-11. The two sales together need 2 shares and user 3 holds 1, so only
one can be executed. The second sale may be refused when it is requested or
rejected when the market opens. Either way it is not executed and the user is

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-17: Queued trade with real prices}\\}

Requirements: FR40

Control: Manual

Initial State: A test user who has acknowledged the simulated market notice
and has enough currency to buy 1 share of a widely traded stock in the live
market, signed in on a test device with the release build of \progname{}.

Input: While the US market is closed, the tester notes the last price
\progname{} shows for the stock and asks to buy 1 share. The tester then keeps
\progname{} closed until at least 30 minutes after the market opens at
9:30~a.m.\ Eastern time on the next trading day, and opens it.

Output: When it is requested, the trade is shown as queued, with a notice that
it will run at the opening price. When \progname{} is opened, the trade is
shown as executed, at a time after 9:30~a.m.\ Eastern time and before the app
was opened, and at a price different from the one noted. The balance has
fallen by that price.

Test Case Derivation: This test exists because the automated tests of FR40,
test-mkt-15 and test-mkt-16, cannot show how the queue behaves with real
prices. In those tests, the test provider sends the first price after the open
when the test chooses. With the real provider, that price arrives whenever the
provider publishes it, and a quote just after the open may still be the last
price from before it. FR40 requires the first price obtained after the market
opens, so a trade run at that older price is the failure this test looks for.
It compares the two prices because a trade that executed after 9:30~a.m.\ at
the older price would otherwise pass. A widely traded stock is used because its
price is very likely to change overnight, so a matching price points to this
failure. The tester waits 30 minutes, which is longer than
\texttt{QUOTE\_STALENESS\_LIMIT} (15 minutes), so an execution time before the
app was opened shows that the backend ran the trade by itself. The test also
checks, on a real device, that the user sees the queued trade and its notice.

How test will be performed: The tester requests the trade in the evening and
notes the stock's last price and the balance. After waiting, the tester checks
the trade's status, time, and price in \progname{}, and the new balance. If the
execution price matches the noted price, the tester repeats the test with
another stock, and a second match fails the test.

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-23: Trading in a market scenario}\\}

Requirements: FR45

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test scenario that replays a market downturn, in which the
closing price of asset A is 100.00 on its first trading day $t_1$ and 90.00
on the next, $t_2$. In the live market, A's current price is 150.00. A test
user with a live balance of 500 who holds 2 shares of A and has acknowledged
the simulated market notice.

Input: The user starts the scenario and buys 10 shares of A on day $t_1$. The

the sale leaves a scenario balance of 9,900 and no shares. Throughout the

undone when the user left would still mix the two portfolios.

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-25: Advancing a scenario}\\}

Requirements: FR47

Control: Automatic

\item{\textbf{test-mkt-27: Removing an asset while its market is closed}\\}

Requirements: FR31

Initial State: An asset in the live market whose market is closed, with a last

40, and their queued sale is rejected, and they are told. After the removal,
the backend asks the test provider for no more prices for the asset.

Test Case Derivation: FR31 sells at the most recent price whether or not the

may never get one. The price is older than \texttt{QUOTE\_STALENESS\_LIMIT} (15

sale, and the run with the market closed checks that FR40 does not queue it. A
queued trade would otherwise wait for an opening price that never comes, so
user 2's purchase is rejected. User 3 has both a holding and a queued sale, so
a balance of 80 would show the share being sold twice. Checking the test
provider's requests confirms that the backend stops tracking the asset, which
is the reason for selling at removal.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sets up the holdings and queued
trades, removes the asset as an administrator, reads each user's balance,
holdings, and trades through the API, and checks the requests recorded by the
test provider.

\item{\textbf{test-learn-3: Feedback on a change in portfolio value}\\}

Requirements: FR52, FR60

Control: Automatic

asset B. Each last viewed their portfolio when A and B were both priced at
50.00, a portfolio value of 1,000.00.

\subsubsection{Social Feed}

These tests cover the social feed requirements in SRS Section 6.5 (FR61 to
FR71). They use four test users, \texttt{alex\_test}, \texttt{bea\_test},
\texttt{cal\_test} and \texttt{dev\_test}, referred to as A, B, C and D. Unless
a test says otherwise, A is friends with B and C and not with D. Automatic
tests act as each user through the API with that user's own session, so every
check is made from the point of view of the user who would see the result.
The times at which updates are shared are set by the backend's test clock.

\begin{enumerate}

\item{\textbf{test-social-1: Sending a friend request}\\}

Requirements: FR61

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A and D are not friends and have no request between them.

Input: A sends a friend request to the username \texttt{dev\_test}.

Output: D's list of received requests contains one pending request from A.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR61 is that entering another
user's username sends them a request that they can see. The check is made
from D's side, which is where the request must appear.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends the request as A and reads
D's received requests as D.

\item{\textbf{test-social-2: Friends only after acceptance}\\}

Requirements: FR62, FR63

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A has no request with C or D. C and D have each shared an
update with all their friends.

Input: A sends a request to D. A then tries to accept that request as A. D
declines it. A sends a request to C, and C accepts it.

Output: While A's request to D is pending, A and D are not friends and A sees
none of D's updates. A's attempt to accept their own request is refused, and
the request stays pending. After D declines, A and D are still not friends,
and the request is no longer in D's list of received requests. After C
accepts, A and C are in each other's friend lists.

Test Case Derivation: FR62 makes two users friends only after the user who
received the request accepts it, so the sender accepting their own request
must not count. FR63 lets the recipient decline, after which the two are not
friends and the decliner no longer sees the request. The accepted request to
C shows that acceptance does create the friendship, so the refusals are not
caused by friendships never forming.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends each action as the user named
in the input and reads the friend lists, received requests and feeds after
each step.

\item{\textbf{test-social-3: Removing a friend}\\}

Requirements: FR64

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A and B are friends. A has shared update $U_1$ with B, and B
has shared update $U_2$ with A.

Input: A removes B as a friend.

Output: $U_1$ is no longer in B's feed, and $U_2$ is no longer in A's feed.
Neither user is in the other's friend list.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR64 is that after the removal,
neither user sees the other's shared updates. Each user has shared one
update, so the test checks both directions; removing only the remover's view
would leave $U_1$ in B's feed.

How test will be performed: A pytest test reads both feeds before and after
the removal.

\item{\textbf{test-social-4: Kinds of update that can be shared}\\}

Requirements: FR65, FR68

Control: Automatic and Manual

Initial State: A has 8,000 steps today, a streak of 12, has reached the 7-day
streak milestone, and has passed the knowledge check on
\textit{Diversification}. A's profile has an age of 25, a height of 170 cm
and a weight of 68 kg, and A has a balance of 450 and a step record of 4,200
steps from 9:00 to 10:00 today.

Input: A shares with B one update of each kind in SRS Table 6.5: their step
total for today, their streak, the 7-day streak milestone, and the
\textit{Diversification} knowledge check. A then sends requests to share their
balance, their portfolio value, their weight, and the 4,200-step record. On a
test device, the tester opens A's share screen.

Output: B's feed contains the four updates, showing 8,000 steps, a streak of
12, the 7-day streak milestone and the \textit{Diversification} knowledge
check. None of them contains A's age, height or weight, or any time of day.
The four other requests are refused and add nothing to B's feed. The share
screen offers only the four kinds of update.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR65 is that the user can share one
update of each kind in SRS Table 6.5 and nothing else, so the test shares
each kind and tries four things outside the table. FR68 (Hazard Analysis,
SEC-35) excludes body measurements and individual activity records, which the
weight and the 4,200-step record test. The Hazard Analysis also excludes the
exact time of an activity (SEC-14), which is why no time of day may appear.
The device check covers the app, since the requests test only the backend.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sends each share request as A and
reads B's feed as B. The tester then records the options on the share screen.

\item{\textbf{test-social-5: Choosing who sees an update}\\}

Requirements: FR66

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A is friends with B, C and D.

Input: A shares update $U_1$ with B only, and update $U_2$ with B and C. C
then requests $U_1$ by its identifier.

Output: B's feed contains $U_1$ and $U_2$. C's feed contains $U_2$ only. D's
feed contains neither. C's request for $U_1$ is refused.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR66 is that an update shared with
selected friends appears in their feeds and no other friend's. D is a friend
who was never selected, which checks that sharing with some friends does not
share with all. C's direct request checks that an unselected friend cannot
get the update another way (Hazard Analysis, SEC-15).

How test will be performed: A pytest test shares both updates as A, reads each
feed as its owner, and sends C's request as C.

\item{\textbf{test-social-6: Preview before sharing}\\}

Requirements: FR67

Control: Manual

Initial State: Two test devices, one signed in as A and one as B, who are
friends. A has activity, a streak, a milestone and a passed knowledge check,
as in test-social-4.

Input: On A's device, the tester starts sharing each of the four kinds of
update with B, takes a screenshot of the preview, and confirms. For a fifth
update, the tester cancels at the preview.

Output: A preview is shown before every update is shared. Each update in B's
feed shows the same values and text as its preview. The cancelled update does
not appear in B's feed.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR67 is that for every update the
preview matches what the selected friends see, so each of the four kinds is
checked on B's own device. Cancelling at the preview checks that the preview
comes before sharing, since an update shared before its preview would already
be in B's feed.

How test will be performed: The tester compares each screenshot with the
matching update on B's device and records any difference.

\item{\textbf{test-social-7: Contents and order of the feed}\\}

Requirements: FR69

Control: Automatic

Initial State: B is friends with A and C and not with D. No updates have been
shared.

Input: A shares $U_1$ with B at 10:00. C shares $U_2$ with B at 10:05. A shares
$U_3$ with C only at 10:10. D shares $U_4$ with all their friends at 10:15. B
then reads their feed.

Output: B's feed contains exactly $U_2$ followed by $U_1$.

Test Case Derivation: FR69 shows every update shared with the user by a
friend, and no others, newest first. $U_1$ and $U_2$ are shared with B by
friends, so both appear, with $U_2$ first because it is newer. $U_3$ is from a
friend but not shared with B, and $U_4$ is from a user who is not B's friend,
so neither appears. Each update is shared at a different time, so only one
order is correct.

How test will be performed: A pytest test sets the backend's clock to each
time, shares each update as its author, and reads B's feed as B.

\item{\textbf{test-social-8: Deleting a shared update}\\}

Requirements: FR70

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A has shared update $U$ with B and C.

Input: A deletes $U$. B then requests $U$ by its identifier.

Output: $U$ is no longer in B's or C's feed, and B's request for $U$ is
refused.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR70 is that a deleted update no
longer appears in any friend's feed, so both friends it was shared with are
checked. The direct request checks that the update was removed and not only
hidden from feeds.

How test will be performed: A pytest test deletes $U$ as A, reads B's and
C's feeds, and sends B's request.

\item{\textbf{test-social-9: No rankings between users}\\}

Requirements: FR71

Control: Manual

Initial State: Test user A is friends with B, C and D, signed in on a test
device. Their totals today are A 20,000 steps, B 12,000, C 1,000 and D 5,000,
and their balances follow the same order. C has shared an update with A at
10:00, B at 10:05 and D at 10:10.

Input: The tester opens every screen in \progname{}'s navigation, including
the friend list and the social feed.

Output: No screen orders users by activity, currency or portfolio
performance, or shows a user's position relative to others, such as ``1st''
or ``top''. No list of A's friends is in the order B, D, C or C, D, B. The
feed lists D's, B's and C's updates in that order.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR71 (Hazard Analysis, SAF-11)
covers every screen. The test data is chosen so that an ordering by activity
or balance can be told apart from every order that is allowed. Sorted by
activity, A's friends are B, D, C (or C, D, B from lowest), which differs from
their alphabetical order, B, C, D. The feed must be newest first (FR69), which
gives D, B, C, so a feed sorted by activity would also stand out. The screens
come from the app's navigation, as in test-mkt-20.

How test will be performed: The tester opens each screen, records the order
in which users appear on any screen that lists more than one, and records any
ranking, position or ``top'' label.

\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Reminders and Settings}

These tests cover the reminders and settings requirements in SRS Section 6.6
(FR72 to FR77). The reminder types are Activity, Milestone, Portfolio,
Learning, and Social, each sent when the event in the SRS's table of reminder
types occurs. A user who has not chosen any reminder periods receives
reminders during \texttt{DEFAULT\_REMINDER\_PERIOD} (9:00 to 20:00 in their
time zone).

Reminders are local notifications that the app creates on the phone, with no
push notification service (SRS Section 2.3). The automated reminder tests
therefore run against the frontend with Jest, with the phone's clock and its
notification service replaced by test versions, so that the time of each
event and of each reminder delivered are known. A reminder period includes
its start time but not its end time, so two periods that meet, such as 9:00
to 12:00 and 12:00 to 15:00, do not both include 12:00.

\begin{enumerate}

\item{\textbf{test-remind-1: Choosing reminder periods}\\}

Requirements: FR72

Control: Manual

Initial State: A test user signed in on a test device, who has not chosen any
reminder periods.

Input: The tester sets two reminder periods, 7:00 to 8:30 and 18:00 to 19:00,
closes \progname{} completely, and opens it again.

Output: The reminder settings show both periods, 7:00 to 8:30 and 18:00 to
19:00.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR72 has the user set a period
and see it in their settings the next time they open the app, so the app is
closed completely, not only sent to the background. FR72 allows more than one
period, so two are set, which checks that saving the second does not replace
the first. Both periods differ from \texttt{DEFAULT\_REMINDER\_PERIOD}, and the
first begins before it, so settings that fell back to the default would be
detected.

How test will be performed: The tester sets the periods in the reminder
settings, closes the app from the phone's app switcher, opens it again, and
checks the periods shown.

\item{\textbf{test-remind-2: Reminders only in the chosen periods}\\}

Requirements: FR73

Control: Automatic

Initial State: Two test users with every reminder type turned on, in a fixed
time zone. User 1 has reminder periods of 7:00 to 8:30 and 18:00 to 19:00.
User 2 has not chosen any periods.

Input: For user 1, friend requests arrive at 6:59, 7:00, 8:29, 8:30, 12:00,
and 19:00, and the event of each other reminder type occurs at 12:00. For
user 2, friend requests arrive at 8:59, 9:00, 19:59, and 20:00. The clock then
runs to the end of the next day.

Output: User 1's Social reminders are delivered at 7:00, 7:00, 8:29, 18:00,
18:00, and 7:00 the next day, and their other reminders at 18:00. User 2's
reminders are delivered at 9:00, 9:00, 19:59, and 9:00 the next day. No
reminder is delivered outside the user's periods.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR73 has one user with chosen
periods and one without, so there are two users, and user 2's reminders
follow \texttt{DEFAULT\_REMINDER\_PERIOD} (9:00 to 20:00). User 1's first
period, and user 2's default period, are each tested at both ends, at the
boundary and one minute before it. A period includes its start but not its
end, so a request at 7:00 or 8:29 is delivered at once, while one at 6:59 or
8:30 is not. The request at 12:00 is between user 1's two periods, and the one
at 19:00 is at the end of the second period, so it is delivered the next day.
An event outside a period is delivered at the start of
the next period, since FR75 requires each event to produce one reminder within
the user's periods. Social reminders are used for the boundaries because a
friend request is still relevant whenever its reminder arrives, so the
expected time depends only on the periods. The other four types each have an
event at 12:00, which checks that every type follows the periods, not only
Social reminders. Their events are chosen so that they still hold at 18:00:
for example, the user records no activity that day, so the Activity reminder
is still due.

How test will be performed: A Jest test sets each user's periods, sets the
test clock to each time and causes each event, runs the clock to the end of
the next day, and records the type and time of every notification sent to the
test notification service.

\item{\textbf{test-remind-3: Turning reminder types off}\\}

Requirements: FR74

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user with the default reminder period and every
reminder type turned on, in a fixed time zone.

Input: The test is run five times, once for each reminder type. In each run,
that type is turned off, and at 12:00 the event of every reminder type occurs.

Output: In each run, the user receives one reminder of each of the four types
left on, and none of the type turned off.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR74 has one type turned off,
which then sends no reminders while the types left on still do. Each type has
its own switch, so each is turned off in its own run. The event of every type
occurs in every run, so a switch that turned off the wrong type, or more than
one type, would show up as a missing reminder of a type left on. The events
are at 12:00, inside \texttt{DEFAULT\_REMINDER\_PERIOD}, so the periods
cannot be why a reminder is missing.

How test will be performed: As in test-remind-2, with one run for each type.

\item{\textbf{test-remind-4: Activity reminders}\\}

Requirements: FR75

Control: Automatic

Initial State: Six test users with the default reminder period and every
reminder type turned on, in a fixed time zone. Their state on day $d$, all
recorded before 9:00, with no activity recorded after it, is:

\begin{itemize}
  \item User 1: a goal of 5,000 steps, 2,000 steps, and a streak of 0.
  \item User 2: a goal of 5,000 steps, 6,000 steps, and a streak of 0.
  \item User 3: as user 1, with day $d$ marked as a rest day.
  \item User 4: no goal, no activity, and a streak of 3.
  \item User 5: no goal, 50 steps, and a streak of 3.
  \item User 6: no goal, no activity, and a streak of 0.
\end{itemize}

Input: The test clock runs through day $d$.

Output: Users 1 and 4 each receive exactly one Activity reminder, between
9:00 and 20:00. Users 2, 3, 5, and 6 receive no Activity reminder.

Test Case Derivation: An Activity reminder is sent when the day is not a rest
day, and either the user has not yet met the day's goal or their streak will
end if they record no activity (SRS Section 6.6). User 1 has only the first
reason, since a streak of 0 cannot end, and user 4 has only the second, since
they have no goal. Each user who gets no reminder differs from one of these
two in one way: user 2 from user 1 by meeting the goal, user 3 from user 1 by
the rest day, user 5 from user 4 by recording activity, which keeps a streak
with no goal (test-act-17), and user 6 from user 4 by having no streak to
lose. FR75 sends one reminder for each event, so a reminder repeated through
the day is also detected.

How test will be performed: A Jest test gives the app each user's goal,
totals, streak, and rest days, runs the test clock through day $d$, and
records each Activity notification sent to the test notification service.

\item{\textbf{test-remind-5: Milestone and Portfolio reminders}\\}

Requirements: FR75

Control: Automatic

Initial State: Five test users with the default reminder period and every
reminder type turned on, in a fixed time zone, on day $d$. User 1 has a goal
of 5,000 steps, no steps yet, and no currency earned. User 2 has no goal and
has earned 990 currency in total. Users 1 and 2 have a streak of 0, so no
streak milestone can be reached. Users 3, 4, and 5 each hold 1 share of a different asset
whose price at the previous day's close was 100.00.

Input: User 1 records 5,000 steps at 10:00 and 2,000 more at 15:00. User 2
records 1,000 steps at 10:00. At 11:00, the prices of the assets held by
users 3, 4, and 5 become 105.00, 105.01, and 94.99.

Output: Users 1 and 2 each receive exactly one Milestone reminder. Users 4
and 5 each receive one Portfolio reminder, and user 3 receives none. All are
delivered between 9:00 and 20:00.

Test Case Derivation: A Milestone reminder is sent when the user meets their
goal or reaches a value in \texttt{MILESTONES} (SRS Section 6.6), so there is
one user for each. User 1 meets the goal at 10:00, and the steps at 15:00
check that meeting it once gives one reminder, not one for each sync. User 2's
1,000 steps earn 10, which brings their total to 1,000, the first currency
value in \texttt{MILESTONES}, at a known time. A Portfolio reminder is sent
when the portfolio value has moved by more than \texttt{FEEDBACK\_THRESHOLD}
(5\%) since the previous day. Each of users 3 to 5 holds one share of one
asset, so the change in value is the change in price: exactly 5\% for user 3,
which is not more than the threshold, and 5.01\% for user 4, which is. User 5's
price falls by 5.01\%, which catches a check that looks only for gains, since
the value has moved by more than the threshold in either direction.

How test will be performed: A Jest test gives the app each event at its time,
in the form of the backend's responses, runs the test clock through day $d$,
and records each notification sent to the test notification service.

\item{\textbf{test-remind-6: Learning and Social reminders}\\}

Requirements: FR75

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user with the default reminder period and every reminder
type turned on, in a fixed time zone, with two friends, F and G.

Input: On day $d$, an administrator publishes a new concept explanation at
10:00, publishes a new knowledge check at 11:00, and edits an existing concept
explanation at 12:00. At 13:00, F shares an update with the user. At 14:00, G
shares an update with F only.

Output: The user receives two Learning reminders, for the new explanation and
the new knowledge check, and one Social reminder, for F's update. All are
delivered between 9:00 and 20:00.

Test Case Derivation: A Learning reminder is sent when a new concept
explanation or knowledge check is published (SRS Section 6.6), so one of each
is published. An edit does not publish new content, so it gives no reminder,
which catches a system that reminds every user each time content is
corrected. A Social reminder is sent when a friend shares an update with the
user or sends a friend request. Friend requests are tested in test-remind-2,
so this test uses a shared update. G chooses to share only with F (FR66), so a
reminder for G's update would tell the user about an update they cannot see.

How test will be performed: As in test-remind-5.

\item{\textbf{test-remind-7: No health data read after consent is
withdrawn}\\}

Requirements: FR76

Control: Automatic

Initial State: A test user who has consented to \progname{} reading their
health data. The phone's health platform is replaced by a test version that
records every read and holds activity records for the current day.

Input: The app syncs. The user then withdraws consent in the app, and new
activity records are added to the test health platform. The app is closed and
opened again, its background sync runs, and the user opens their activity
trends.

Output: The test health platform records reads during the first sync and none
after the withdrawal. The app sends no activity totals to the backend after
the withdrawal.

Test Case Derivation: The fit criterion of FR76 requires that no further
health data be read after the user withdraws consent in the app. Withdrawing
consent in the app does not remove \progname{}'s permission in the phone's
settings, and on iOS the app cannot remove it, so the health platform would
still answer reads. The app must stop reading on its own, so the test watches
the app's reads, not the permission. The sync before the withdrawal shows that
the test platform records reads, so the test cannot pass only because no read
was ever recorded. After the withdrawal, the test tries each way the app can
read health data: opening the app, the background sync, and the activity
screen. The new records give the app something to read.

How test will be performed: A Jest test replaces the health platform and the
backend's activity endpoint with test versions, runs the steps, and checks the
reads and requests each one records.

\item{\textbf{test-remind-8: Health permission list}\\}

Requirements: FR77

Control: Manual

Initial State: Two new test users, one on an Android test device and one on
an iOS test device, each with the release build of \progname{}.

Input: On each device, when \progname{} asks for health permissions, the
tester allows steps and workouts and denies wheelchair pushes. The tester then
opens the health permission list in \progname{}'s settings. On Android, the
tester then allows wheelchair pushes in Health Connect and opens the list
again.

Output:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Android: every requested data type is listed with the feature that
    uses it. Steps and workouts are marked as allowed and wheelchair pushes as
    not allowed, as in Health Connect. After pushes are allowed, they are
    marked as allowed.
  \item iOS: every requested data type is listed with the feature that uses
    it, with a statement that iOS does not tell apps which types were allowed
    and where to review them in the Health settings. No type is marked as
    allowed or not allowed.
\end{itemize}

Test Case Derivation: FR77's fit criterion has one case for each platform,
since Health Connect reports which types an app may read and HealthKit does
not (SRS Section 6.6), so there is one device for each. The tester allows
some types and denies one, which is the case where the list can be wrong: a
list of the requested types shown as allowed would mark pushes as allowed on
Android. Pushes are denied because they are the type a user who does not use
a wheelchair is most likely to deny. Allowing pushes afterwards checks that
the Android list reads the current permissions, not the answers given at
setup, since users can change them at any time. On iOS, \progname{} cannot
know which types were allowed, so any type marked as allowed or not allowed
would be a guess.

How test will be performed: On Android, the tester compares the list with
\progname{}'s permissions in Health Connect. On iOS, the tester checks the
statement and follows the directions to confirm they lead to \progname{}'s
permissions in the Health settings.

\end{enumerate}

with pytest in \texttt{test/backend}, and app units with Jest, on every pull
request.

\subsubsection{Accounts and Profile}

These tests check the account rules on the backend, below the API, so that a
failure points to the rule that caused it rather than to the request.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{ut-acct-1: Password length.} A password of 7 characters is
    refused and one of 8 is accepted, testing \texttt{MIN\_PASSWORD\_LENGTH}
    (8) on both sides (Hazard Analysis, SEC-38).
  \item \textbf{ut-acct-2: Age confirmation.} Sign-up details with the age
    confirmation true are accepted. False and missing are both refused (FR2).
  \item \textbf{ut-acct-3: Username in use.} Checking \texttt{sam\_test} when
    an account already uses it reports it as taken, and checking
    \texttt{jordan\_test} reports it as free (FR3).
  \item \textbf{ut-acct-4: Stored passwords.} After sign-up with the password
    \texttt{Walk2026run}, the stored value is not equal to the password,
    checking the stored value against the password succeeds, and checking it
    against \texttt{Walk2026ruN} fails (FR4).
  \item \textbf{ut-acct-5: Reset links.} A reset link works once for the
    account it was issued to. Using it a second time, or for another account,
    is refused (FR6).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Learning and Feedback}

The feedback rules are calculations on a portfolio, so they are tested as
functions with portfolios given directly, with no database or API. Each test
has a hand-calculated expected value.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{ut-learn-1: Feedback threshold.} With a value of 1,000.00 at
    the last view, a new value of 1,050.00 (a change of exactly
    \texttt{FEEDBACK\_THRESHOLD}, 5\%) gives no feedback, and 1,050.01 and
    949.99 both give feedback (FR52).
  \item \textbf{ut-learn-2: Largest change.} For changes of $+70$ in A and
    $-10$ in B, A is named. For $0$ in A and $-70$ in B, B is named (FR52).
  \item \textbf{ut-learn-3: Shares of the portfolio.} For holdings worth
    100.00, 100.00 and 100.00, each share is one third, and the shares add up
    to 100\% within \texttt{VALUE\_TOLERANCE} (0.01). For a single holding, its
    share is 100\% (FR53).
  \item \textbf{ut-learn-4: Concentration limit.} Holdings worth 500.00 and
    500.00 (exactly \texttt{CONCENTRATION\_LIMIT}, 50\%) give no explanation,
    and 501.00 and 499.00 give one (FR54).
  \item \textbf{ut-learn-5: Grading.} For a check with answers b, a and c, the
    answers b, c and c give questions 1 and 3 correct and question 2
    incorrect. All correct and all wrong are also tested (FR56).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Social Feed}

The visibility rule decides every check in the social feed system tests, so
it is tested on its own for every combination of its inputs.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{ut-social-1: Visibility.} An update is visible to a viewer
    exactly when the viewer is its author, or is both a selected viewer and
    currently a friend of the author. The test checks all four combinations of
    selected or not and friend or not, and the author (FR64, FR66; Hazard
    Analysis, SEC-13).
  \item \textbf{ut-social-2: Friend requests.} A pending request becomes
    accepted only when its recipient accepts it, and declined when its
    recipient declines it. Accepting by the sender, and accepting a declined
    request, are refused (FR62, FR63).
  \item \textbf{ut-social-3: Building an update.} Each of the four kinds in
    SRS Table 6.5 builds an update, and any other kind is refused. No built
    update contains an age, height, weight, individual record, location or
    time of day (FR65, FR68; Hazard Analysis, SEC-14).
  \item \textbf{ut-social-4: Feed order.} Updates shared at 10:00, 10:05 and
    10:10 are returned newest first, whatever order they are given in (FR69).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Usability Survey Questions} \label{SecUsabilitySurvey}

% \wss{This is a section that would be appropriate for some projects.}

Participants use \progname{} on their own phones for 4 to 7 days, as part of
their normal routine, rather than in one supervised session. The study uses
the version of \progname{} from the Revision~0 demonstration and tests its core
loop (Development Plan, Section~9): earning virtual currency from steps, buying
shares of stocks at real prices, and following the portfolio's value. Shares
are bought whole, and steps earn 1 unit of currency per 100 steps (SRS,
\texttt{EARNING\_RATES}), so the stock list used in the study includes at least
five stocks priced under 50 USD when the study starts. A participant can then
afford a share after about a day of walking. Two
of the features being tested span multiple days: earning rewards each day and
seeing a portfolio's value change as prices move. A longer study is therefore
needed to test them. The study includes at least three days when the stock
market is open, so that participants can buy shares and see prices change. The
participant profile and the study procedure are given in
Section~\ref{SecSoftwareValidation}.

\subsubsection{Before-Study Questionnaire} \label{SecBeforeStudy}

Participants complete this questionnaire on Day~1, after they agree to take
part and before they install \progname{}. It has three parts: background,
attitude to risk, and the knowledge check for G1. Answers are recorded under
the participant ID, and the form asks for no name, contact details, or health
measurements.

\paragraph{Part A: Background.}

{\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{A\arabic{enumi}.}
\renewcommand{\theenumi}{A\arabic{enumi}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Are you 18 years old or older?
    \begin{choices}
      \item[$\circ$] Yes
      \item[$\circ$] No
    \end{choices}
  \item Which kind of phone will you use during the study?
    \begin{choices}
      \item[$\circ$] Android
      \item[$\circ$] iPhone
    \end{choices}
  \item Which statement best describes your experience with investing?
    \begin{choices}

      \item[$\circ$] I have invested a little, for example in stocks or a fund

\paragraph{Part B: Attitude to risk.}

How willing a participant is to take risks is likely to shape how they use
their portfolio. People often describe themselves differently from how they
act, so these questions describe everyday situations instead of asking about
risk directly. They were written for this study, based on the kinds of
questions in a published risk tolerance scale~\citep{GrableLytton1999}. The
Usability Report compares these answers with what the participant shows and
tells the interviewer in the debrief (I7).

On the form, this part is titled ``Your money choices'', so that the title
does not say what is being measured. Participants see this instruction before
the questions:

\begin{quote}
There are no right or wrong answers. For each question, choose what you would
most likely do.
\end{quote}

{\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{B\arabic{enumi}.}
\renewcommand{\theenumi}{B\arabic{enumi}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Imagine you have saved \$1,000 that you will not need for five years.
    You can keep it in a savings account, which pays a small amount of
    guaranteed interest, or invest it in something such as stocks, whose value
    can rise or fall. What would you do with it?
    \begin{choices}
      \item[$\circ$] Keep all of it in the savings account
      \item[$\circ$] Keep most of it in the savings account and invest the rest
      \item[$\circ$] Invest most of it and keep the rest in the savings account
      \item[$\circ$] Invest all of it
    \end{choices}
  \item Imagine you invested \$1,000 in the stock market, and a month later it
    is worth \$800. What would you most likely do?
    \begin{choices}
      \item[$\circ$] Sell all of it
      \item[$\circ$] Sell some of it
      \item[$\circ$] Keep all of it and wait
      \item[$\circ$] Keep all of it and invest more
    \end{choices}
  \item You can put \$1,000 into one of these for a year. Which would you
    choose?
    \begin{choices}
      \item[$\circ$] One that will be worth between \$1,000 and \$1,050
      \item[$\circ$] One that will be worth between \$900 and \$1,200
      \item[$\circ$] One that will be worth between \$600 and \$1,600
    \end{choices}
\end{enumerate}}

On Day~1, participants save a link to the log on their phone's home screen, so

{\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{L\arabic{enumi}.}
\renewcommand{\theenumi}{L\arabic{enumi}}

      \item[$\circ$] Letting \progname{} read my steps
      \item[$\circ$] My currency or rewards
      \item[$\circ$] Buying shares
      \item[$\circ$] My portfolio
      \item[$\circ$] Something else, such as installing the app

\subsubsection{After-Study Questionnaire} \label{SecAfterStudy}

Participants complete this questionnaire the day after the last day of the
study, just before the debrief. It has no written questions, since the debrief
covers what worked and what did not. Its parts continue from the before-study
questionnaire.

\paragraph{Part D: Core tasks.}

These four tasks, which follow the core loop in order, are the core tasks for
this study. Participants are given them on Day~1 as things to try during the
study. For each task, participants choose one of these answers:

\begin{choices}
  \item[$\circ$] Yes, without help from outside the app
  \item[$\circ$] Yes, but I needed help from outside the app at least once
  \item[$\circ$] No, I tried but could not
  \item[$\circ$] No, I did not try
\end{choices}

{\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{D\arabic{enumi}.}
\renewcommand{\theenumi}{D\arabic{enumi}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Let \progname{} read your steps.
  \item See the currency you earned from your steps.
  \item Buy shares of a stock.
  \item Check how much your portfolio is worth.
\end{enumerate}}

A participant completes the core tasks if they choose the first answer for all
four, and none of their log entries shows outside help with one of them. Help
from the team with installing the app, or with a problem on the team's side
such as the server being down, is not outside help. G2 and NFR2 are met if at
least \texttt{MIN\_TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE} (80\%) of beginners complete the core
tasks.

\paragraph{Part E: Larger text.}

On one weekday of the study, the team sends each participant the steps for
their kind of phone to set its text size to the setting closest to
\texttt{MAX\_TEXT\_SCALE} (200\%) of the default. It asks them to buy at least
one share and check their portfolio that day.

{\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{E\arabic{enumi}.}
\renewcommand{\theenumi}{E\arabic{enumi}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item On the day you used larger text, could you still read everything and
    use every button in \progname{}?
    \begin{choices}
      \item[$\circ$] Yes
      \item[$\circ$] Mostly, but some text or buttons were cut off or hidden
      \item[$\circ$] No, I could not use some screens
      \item[$\circ$] I did not use larger text
    \end{choices}
\end{enumerate}}

NFR8 is met if at least one participant on each kind of phone used larger
text, and no participant reports a problem on a screen used for a core task.

\paragraph{Part F: Knowledge check again.}

Participants answer C1 to C6 again, with the same instruction as before, and
then one more question:

{\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{F\arabic{enumi}.}
\renewcommand{\theenumi}{F\arabic{enumi}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item During the study, did you look up the answer to any of these questions,
    or ask anyone about them?
    \begin{choices}
      \item[$\circ$] Yes
      \item[$\circ$] No
    \end{choices}
\end{enumerate}}

A participant who answers Yes, or who had full marks before the study, is

\subsubsection{Debrief Interview} \label{SecDebrief}

The debrief takes about 30 minutes, in person or by video call. One team
member asks the questions and a second takes notes. The interviewer asks open
questions and does not explain how the app works until the end, so that the
answers describe the participant's own experience.
A question that starts with a condition in italics is asked only when the
condition applies, and is skipped for anything the participant has already
explained.

{\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{I\arabic{enumi}.}
\renewcommand{\theenumi}{I\arabic{enumi}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Walk me through how you used \progname{} during the study.
  \item What worked well for you, and why?
  \item What was the most confusing or frustrating moment? What did you expect
    to happen instead?
  \item \emph{For each log entry where L2 was not ``It went well'' or L4 was not
    ``No'':} You wrote that \ldots{} Can you tell me more about that?
  \item \emph{For each Part~D task not answered ``Yes, without help from
    outside the app'':} What happened with this task?
  \item \emph{If E1 was not ``Yes'' or ``I did not use larger text'':} Which
    screens had the problem, and what happened on them?
  \item Can you show me your portfolio? How did you decide what to do with
    your currency, and what, if anything, did you do as prices moved?
  \item What, if anything, did you learn from using \progname{}?
  \item \emph{If A3 was ``I invest regularly'':} How does \progname{} compare
    with how you invest now, and what do you think a beginner would find hard?
  \item If you could change one thing about \progname{}, what would it be?
\end{enumerate}}

After I10, the interviewer goes through the knowledge check answers with the
participant and answers their questions. The Usability Report groups the notes
by step of the core loop, in the same way as the log entries.
