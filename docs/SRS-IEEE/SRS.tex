This document is the Software Requirements Specification (SRS) for
\progname{}, a mobile application in which young adults earn virtual currency
from the physical activity their phone records and invest it in a simulated
market that follows real-world prices. It states what \progname{} must do and
the qualities it must have, without prescribing how it will be built. The SRS
is the reference against which the development team and the client agree on
scope, the basis for the design, and the source from which the Verification
and Validation (V\&V) Plan derives its tests. It also records the safety and
security requirements identified in the Hazard Analysis (HA), so that design
and testing treat them like every other requirement.

The structure of this document follows the course SRS template, which is based
on ISO/IEC/IEEE~29148, the international standard for requirements
engineering~\citep{ISOIECIEEE29148_2018}, and is consistent with the system
life-cycle processes of ISO/IEC/IEEE~15288~\citep{ISOIECIEEE15288_2023}. This
section states the purpose and scope of \progname{}, identifies its
stakeholders, defines the terms used throughout the document, and describes
the knowledge expected of its readers.

\subsection{Purpose}

% \plt{State the purpose of the system.}

% \plt{The purpose should be in the context of the project itself, not in the
%   context of this course. Although the ``purpose'' of the document is to get a
%   grade, you should not mention this.  Instead, ``fake it'' as if this is a real
%   project.}

% \progname{} is intended to solve ... \plt{What problem does your program solve?
%   The description here should be in the problem space, not the solution space.}

\progname{} is intended to help young adults build fitness and financial
habits together, at the point in life when starting either one pays off most.
It rewards the physical activity users already do with virtual currency, lets
them invest that currency in a simulated market at real-world prices, explains
financial and fitness concepts where they appear, and lets them share the
progress they choose with friends. Because the currency has no real-world
value, users can learn by making investing decisions and seeing their results
without risking money. The problem that motivates \progname{} is described in
detail in the Problem Statement in Section~2.

The same habits also matter beyond the individual user. Canadian guidelines
recommend that adults accumulate at least 150 minutes of moderate to vigorous
physical activity each week~\citep{CSEP2020}, and the national financial
literacy strategy aims for a Canada in which everyone can build financial
resilience~\citep{FCAC2021}. By making both habits approachable and
low-risk, \progname{} is intended to contribute in a small way to both goals.

\progname{} is a single product: a smartphone application for Android and iOS,
together with the server that supports it.

\paragraph{Problem being solved.} Young adults put off building fitness and
investing habits because investing advice is confusing and learning by doing
risks real money, while beginners at exercise lose motivation before they see
results.

\paragraph{Intended users.} Adults aged 18 or older, most of them students or
people in their first jobs, with little or no investing experience and any
level of fitness (Section~\ref{SecUserCharacteristics}).

\paragraph{Major functionality.} Within scope, \progname{}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item reads activity records from the phone's health platform and converts
        them into virtual currency;
  \item lets users buy and sell shares of a curated list of real-world stocks and
        exchange-traded funds (ETFs) at prices that follow the real market;
  \item shows portfolio value, activity trends, and milestones over time;
  \item explains financial and fitness concepts where they appear, gives
        feedback on the user's own decisions, and tracks learning progress through
        knowledge checks;
  \item replays past market periods, such as a downturn, as practice scenarios
        that do not affect the user's live portfolio;
  \item shares the statistics and milestones that the user selects with accepted
        friends.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Expected benefits.} Users practice investing with no financial risk,
stay motivated to exercise because activity has a visible use, and learn core
concepts by applying them to their own decisions. The targets that measure
these benefits are stated as non-functional requirements.

\paragraph{Out of scope.} \progname{} does not:
\begin{itemize}
  \item handle real money: the currency cannot be bought, withdrawn, or
        exchanged, and \progname{} never connects to a bank or brokerage account;
  \item give financial advice: it never recommends buying or selling a
        real-world security;
  \item give medical or training advice: it does not diagnose conditions,
        prescribe exercise programs, or set diet or weight goals;
  \item integrate directly with wearable devices (a stretch goal), since all
        activity is read through the phone's health platform;
  \item offer every listed security, or options, futures, short selling, or
        borrowing to invest;
  \item update prices tick by tick: simulated prices follow real prices with a
        bounded delay, because market data providers limit how often prices can be
        requested;
  \item predict future prices: scenarios replay only historical data;
  \item support public posts, direct messages, or rankings between users;
  \item run on tablets, the web, or desktop computers.
\end{itemize}

These limits also bound the inputs \progname{} must handle. For example, a
day's step count ranges from zero on a rest day to tens of thousands for a very
active user, and every asset price is a positive amount reported by the market
data provider.

\paragraph{Standards and regulations.} The requirements are written to comply
with the following external standards and regulations, which also define the
formats of the data \progname{} exchanges:
{\sloppy
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Privacy:} the Personal Information Protection and Electronic
        Documents Act (PIPEDA)~\citep{PIPEDA}, which governs the collection, use, and
        deletion of users' personal and health data.
  \item \textbf{Accessibility:} the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)
        2.2~\citep{WCAG22}, applied to the app's screens.
  \item \textbf{Security:} the OWASP Mobile Application Security Verification
        Standard (MASVS)~\citep{OWASPMASVS}.
  \item \textbf{Quality characteristics:} the product quality model of ISO/IEC
        25010~\citep{ISOIEC25010_2023}, which names the categories used for the
        non-functional requirements.
  \item \textbf{Data formats:} JavaScript Object Notation (JSON)~\citep{RFC8259}
        for data exchanged between the app, the server, and the market data provider;
        ISO~8601~\citep{ISO8601_2019} for dates, times, and time zones; and
        ISO~4217~\citep{ISO4217_2015} for the currency in which each asset price is
        quoted.
\end{itemize}
\par}

The intended readers of this SRS are the \progname{} development team, the
client, course reviewers, and any future maintainer: the people who will
review the requirements and design software to meet them. The end users of
\progname{} are a different group and are described in
Section~\ref{SecUserCharacteristics}. A reader is expected to have:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Software engineering:} third-year undergraduate knowledge of
        software requirements, including functional and non-functional requirements,
        use cases, traceability, and verification, at the level of a course such as
        SFWRENG 3RA3 (Software Requirements and Security Considerations).
  \item \textbf{Mathematics:} high-school algebra and percentages, enough to
        follow how rewards and portfolio values are calculated.
  \item \textbf{Mobile applications:} everyday familiarity with smartphone
        applications, including installing an app and responding to permission
        prompts. No mobile development experience is needed.
  \item \textbf{Finance and fitness:} no prior knowledge. Every financial and
        fitness term the requirements rely on is defined in
        Table~\ref{tab:DefinitionsAndAcronyms}.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

% Template prompt (hidden): Current situation, problem, impact, desired
% improvement. You will likely borrow some text from your problem description
% document.

\textbf{Current situation.} Building good fitness and financial habits early
pays off for decades, because both compound over time. Regular exercise builds
on itself, and so does money invested early. Poor habits compound in the same
way. Yet many young adults, particularly students and those starting their
first jobs, struggle to begin either one.

\textbf{Problem.} Advice on managing money is scattered across many sources,
which often contradict each other, and learning to invest by doing is risky
because beginners' mistakes cost real money. Starting to exercise is similarly
daunting, and many beginners lose motivation before they see results.

\textbf{Impact.} Many people put off both habits until later, when poor habits
are already established and harder to change.

\textbf{Desired improvement.} An approachable, low-risk way for beginners to
build fitness and financial habits together while staying motivated.

\progname{} is a mobile app in which users earn virtual currency from their
everyday physical activity and invest it in a simulated market that follows
real-world prices. It lets beginners build fitness habits and learn to invest
without risking real money. Its goals, which the requirements in this document
discuss, are:

\begin{description}
  \item[G1 (Learning by doing).] Users learn core financial and fitness
  concepts through hands-on, low-risk practice, with each decision paired with
  feedback explaining what happened and why. \textit{Measured by:} a
  before-and-after knowledge check on a defined set of core concepts, where at
  least 70\% of usability test participants improve their score.
  \item[G2 (A human-centered, all-in-one experience).] Users see their fitness
  progress and financial learning together in one place, so they rarely need
  to leave the app to understand what they are looking at. \textit{Measured
  by:} usability testing, where at least 80\% of first-time participants
  complete the core tasks without outside help.
  \item[G3 (Motivation through friends, with privacy).] Users stay motivated by
  sharing their progress with friends, while keeping control of their data.
  \textit{Measured by:} users control who sees each shared statistic, and no
  raw health data is shared unless the user selects it.
  \item[G4 (Risk-free investing practice).] Users practice investing in a
  simulated market based on real-world market data, without risking real
  money. \textit{Measured by:} users can trade at least 50 real-world stocks at
  prices within 15 minutes of the real market, and can practice in market
  scenarios such as a replay of a past market downturn.
  \item[G5 (Rewards for everyday activity).] Users are rewarded for the
  activities they already do, as recorded by their phone's health platform.
  \textit{Measured by:} at least five activity types are supported.
\end{description}

The responsibilities of each entity in Figure~\ref{Fig_SystemContext} are:

\begin{itemize}
\item User Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
\item Confirm truthfully that they are at least \texttt{MIN\_USER\_AGE} years
  old.
\item Keep any profile measurements they choose to enter, such as height and
  weight, accurate.
\item Grant \progname{} the permissions its features use, and keep an activity
  source recording to the phone's health platform.
\item Make their own trade decisions. \progname{} gives no financial advice.
\item Choose what to share, with which friends, and when to receive reminders.
\item Keep an Internet connection for trading and the social feed.
\item Keep their password private and keep access to their account's email
  address.
\end{itemize}
\item \progname{} Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
\item Award currency only for activity within plausibility limits and up to a
  daily cap (Hazard Analysis SAF-7, SEC-1).
\item Treat a day with no activity data as a day with zero activity.
\item Reject purchases that cost more than the user's balance and sales of
  more shares than the user holds (Hazard Analysis SEC-5).
\item Record each trade once and completely (Hazard Analysis SEC-5, SEC-6).
\item Show each asset price with the time it was obtained, and mark delayed
  prices (Hazard Analysis SEC-2).
\item Refuse trades on out-of-date prices while the market is open, and queue
  trades requested while it is closed (Hazard Analysis SEC-3, SEC-4).
\item Show each shared update only to the friends the user chose, without
  location detail (Hazard Analysis SEC-13, SEC-14).
\item Request only the permissions its features use, explaining each one
  (Hazard Analysis SEC-7, SEC-8).
\item Send the market data provider only the symbols of the assets being
  priced, and no personal or health data (Hazard Analysis SEC-11).
\item Send the email service no personal or health data other than the user's
  email address and their password reset link (Hazard Analysis SEC-11).
\end{itemize}
\item Health Platform Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
\item Store activity records from the user's activity sources, and provide
  the records for the activity types the user has permitted.
\item Grant or withdraw \progname{}'s read access as the user chooses, and
  report the current permission status.
\item Keep the activity data it records as accurate as its activity sources
  allow.
\end{itemize}
\item Market Data Provider Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
\item Supply current and historical prices for the assets \progname{}
  requests, within its request limits.
\end{itemize}
\item Email Service Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
\item Deliver each password reset link that \progname{} sends to the user's
  email address.
\end{itemize}
\item Administrator Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
\item Keep the list of tradable assets current.
\item Keep concept explanations and knowledge checks accurate, and name no
  asset for the user to buy or sell in them (Hazard Analysis SAF-9).
\end{itemize}
\item Friend Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
\item Accept or decline a user's friend request.
\item View the updates a user has shared with them in their social feed.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\progname{} is intended for personal education and habit building. Members of
the general public use it on their own smartphones to track their activity,
earn currency and practice investing in a simulated market. It is not intended
for professional, engineering or scientific use.

\progname{} is not mission-critical: it holds and moves no real money, so an
outage only delays rewards and learning. It is not safety-critical either
since it controls no physical equipment and provides
no medical function. However, it handles personal health data and
is designed to influence how users exercise and make financial decisions. These
hazards, and the requirements that control them, are covered in the Hazard
Analysis. The rationale in Section~\ref{SecRationale} therefore focuses on
those requirements.

\subsection{User Characteristics} \label{SecUserCharacteristics}

% Template prompt (hidden): Summarize the knowledge and skills expected of the
% user, specifically and unambiguously. The user is a different role from the
% intended reader (Section 1.5). Usability requirements are measured against
% this typical user.

The typical user of \progname{} is a young adult, such as a student or someone
starting their first job (Problem Statement and Goals, Section~1). The user of
\progname{}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Is at least \texttt{MIN\_USER\_AGE} years old (Hazard Analysis CA2).
  \item Uses a smartphone daily, and can install an app, grant or deny app
  permissions, and use common mobile app controls.
  \item Carries an Android or iOS phone during everyday activity. No wearable
  device or fitness expertise is required.
  \item Reads English, and has the numeracy of Grade~9 mathematics: working
  with percentages, comparing values, and reading a simple line chart.
  \item Needs no prior knowledge of investing terms such as stock,
  exchange-traded fund (ETF) or portfolio.
\end{itemize}

As per interviews conducted, participants mainly track their activity with a phone. Both
already knew some investing concepts, through self-study or formal study, and
both learned most by doing.
No one interviewed was a self-reported complete beginner, so the beginner characteristics above come
from the Problem Statement, and usability testing should include beginners.

\subsection{System Constraints}

% Template prompts (hidden): System constraints limit the developers' options
% in the design and identify how the system must fit into the world. This is
% the only place in the SRS where design decisions can be specified, so they
% should only be included if they are truly required. Mention platform

The following constraints limit the design of \progname{}. Each one is imposed
by the project brief, an external service, the law or the app stores.

\begin{itemize}

\item[SC\refstepcounter{scnum}\thescnum \label{SC_Platform}:] \progname{} shall
run as a mobile app on Android phones running Android
\texttt{MIN\_ANDROID\_\allowbreak VERSION} or later and on iPhones running iOS
\texttt{MIN\_IOS\_\allowbreak VERSION} or later.
\textit{Rationale:} This project calls for a mobile app, and these are the
versions on the devices available to the team and the supervisor for testing
(Development Plan, Section~10).

\item[SC\refstepcounter{scnum}\thescnum \label{SC_HealthPlatform}:]
\progname{} shall obtain recorded activity only through the phone's health
platform: Apple HealthKit on iOS and Health Connect on Android. This rule also
covers any library \progname{} uses to read health data. No core feature shall
require a wearable device.
\textit{Rationale:} Users carry their phone during everyday activity
(Section~\ref{SecUserCharacteristics}), and the health platforms already
collect data from the phone and from any wearable the user owns (note, wearable
integration is a stretch goal).

\item[SC\refstepcounter{scnum}\thescnum \label{SC_HealthAccess}:] \progname{}
shall access health data only under the access rules Apple (iOS provider) and Google (Android OS provider) set for
their health platforms, such as declaring each data type it reads and
explaining each permission to the user \citep{AppleHealthKit,
GoogleHealthConnect}.
\textit{Rationale:} Apps that break these rules are not accepted in the app
stores. The rules also limit what \progname{} can read, such as how far back
past activity data can be read.

\item[SC\refstepcounter{scnum}\thescnum \label{SC_MarketData}:] \progname{}
shall obtain real-world asset prices from a third-party market data provider
(\texttt{MARKET\_DATA\_PROVIDER}), within the provider's request limits and
licence terms.
\textit{Rationale:} The team cannot produce market prices itself, so request
limits, cost and permitted use are set by the provider.

\item[SC\refstepcounter{scnum}\thescnum \label{SC_NoRealMoney}:] Virtual
currency and simulated holdings shall have no monetary value. They cannot be
bought with, redeemed for or exchanged for money, and \progname{} shall not
connect to any bank or brokerage account or give personalized investment
advice.
\textit{Rationale:} Handling real money or giving personalized investment advice would bring
\progname{} under financial regulation that the team is not licensed for.
Keeping real money out also keeps the practice risk-free (G4).

\item[SC\refstepcounter{scnum}\thescnum \label{SC_Privacy}:] \progname{} shall
collect, use, store and delete personal and health data in line with
Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA)
and the health-data policies of the Apple App Store and Google Play.
\textit{Rationale:} These laws and policies apply to any app that handles this
data in Canada and in these stores. The specific controls are in
Section~\ref{SecPrivacyRetention} and the Hazard Analysis (SEC-7 to SEC-12 and
SEC-18).

\item[SC\refstepcounter{scnum}\thescnum \label{SC_Server}:] Trading, market
prices and the social feed shall require an Internet connection to
\progname{}'s server.
\textit{Rationale:} Prices are fetched from the provider once for all users to
stay within its request limits (\scref{SC_MarketData}), friends' shared updates
must reach other users' phones, and a user's portfolio must survive the loss of
their phone.

\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Activity and motivation}

{\sloppy
  \noindent\begin{itemize}
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_EarnFromActivity}:] Users shall be rewarded for the physical activity they already do, without having to start, stop, or record it themselves.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH2. \emph{Evidence:} Both participants track activity with their phone (P01, P02). Tracking should be ``as touchless as possible'': an app that must be started and stopped by hand is easily left running for hours and gives wrong results (P01). Approximate step counts are acceptable to users (P01). \emph{Goal:} G5.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_UsefulRewards}:] Rewards for activity shall have a visible use to the user beyond the activity count itself.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH2. \emph{Evidence:} A long step streak gives one participant nothing but pride (P01); rewards have not changed the other's habits, although a tangible reward might make them work out more (P02). \emph{Goal:} G5.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_AllAbilities}:] Users of any fitness level or physical ability shall be able to earn rewards, whatever kinds of activity they do.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH6. \emph{Evidence:} Participants' activity goes well beyond walking: basketball, skating, and pickup hockey (P01); soccer, weightlifting, climbing stairs, and rock climbing (P02). The target users have any level of fitness (Problem Statement). \emph{Goal:} G5.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_OwnGoals}:] Users shall choose their own activity goals, or set none, and shall not be pressured to raise them.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1. \emph{Evidence:} One participant has kept the same step goal for over a year, because a higher one takes too much time (P01); the other sets no goals and keeps only a mental check that they ``at least did something'' (P02). \emph{Goal:} G5.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_SafeMotivation}:] Features that motivate activity shall not encourage users to exercise when it is unsafe, such as late at night, while injured, or beyond a healthy amount.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH6. \emph{Evidence:} A streak now makes one participant go out walking at 9~p.m.\ to reach their goal (P01); the other admires a friend for whom ``it doesn't matter how much anything hurts'' (P02). \emph{Goal:} G5.
\end{itemize}
  \par}

\paragraph{Investing practice}

{\sloppy
  \noindent\begin{itemize}
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_RiskFree}:] Users shall be able to practice investing without any risk to real money.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH2. \emph{Evidence:} One participant was well-versed in financial math from university courses but did not know how to act on it until they had hands-on experience with real investments (P02); the other first learned by tracking their own transactions by hand (P01). \emph{Goal:} G4.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_RealMarket}:] Investing practice shall follow the real market closely enough that its lessons carry over to real investing.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH2. \emph{Evidence:} The client's vision is a portfolio that follows real-world prices (Problem Statement). \emph{Goal:} G4.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_PastMarkets}:] Users shall be able to practice in past market conditions, such as a downturn, without affecting their own progress.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH2. \emph{Evidence:} Practicing a downturn safely prepares users for one with real money (Problem Statement). \emph{Goal:} G4.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_NotAdvice}:] \progname{} shall never give, or be mistaken for, advice on what to do with real money.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH6, SH9. \emph{Evidence:} Self-taught investors learn from YouTube and Reddit, where it is easy to follow ``the wrong kinds of investing YouTubers,'' and look for answers tailored to their own situation (P01). Users may act on whatever the app appears to recommend. \emph{Goal:} G4.
  \end{itemize}
  \par}

\paragraph{Learning}

{\sloppy
  \noindent\begin{itemize}
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_LearnInContext}:] Users shall be able to understand each financial and fitness concept where it appears, without leaving \progname{}.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1. \emph{Evidence:} Beginners are told that investing requires studying measures such as price-to-earnings ratios, which confuses them (P01); advice is scattered across sources that contradict each other (Problem Statement). \emph{Goal:} G1, G2.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_LearnByDoing}:] Users shall learn through activities they take part in, not only by reading or watching.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1. \emph{Evidence:} One participant has trouble learning from videos and needs ``a component that's interactive''; watching alone ``doesn't stick'' (P02). The other learned investing by doing it themselves (P01). \emph{Goal:} G1.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_LearnFromDecisions}:] Users shall be able to find out why their portfolio changed after their own decisions, including how diversified it is.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH2. \emph{Evidence:} One participant considers buying individual stocks ``almost like gambling'' compared with diversified index investing (P01); users need to see this in their own results. \emph{Goal:} G1.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_CanadianContext}:] Users shall be able to learn how the concepts they practice apply to saving and investing in Canada, such as tax-free savings accounts.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH6. \emph{Evidence:} Both participants named Canada's account types, such as Tax-Free Savings Accounts (TFSAs) and Registered Retirement Savings Plans (RRSPs), as important things they had to learn (P01, P02); ``so many people aren't doing anything at all'' with their savings (P02). \emph{Goal:} G1.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_SeeProgress}:] Users shall be able to see how their activity and their investments have changed over time, in one place.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1. \emph{Evidence:} One participant checks their heart rate often, out of curiosity and to see that it is healthy (P02); the other tracks both their steps and their portfolio, in separate tools (P01). \emph{Goal:} G2.
  \end{itemize}
  \par}

\paragraph{Sharing and privacy} 

{\sloppy
  \noindent\begin{itemize}
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_ShareProgress}:] Users shall be able to share the progress they choose with friends.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH5. \emph{Evidence:} Being ahead of friends ``feels great,'' and good friends help those who fall behind (P02). \emph{Goal:} G3.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_ControlSharing}:] Users shall control exactly what their friends can see, and nothing shall be shared without the user's choice.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH5. \emph{Evidence:} Removing names does not make a person anonymous, because the stories they tell and other context can reveal who they are (P01). \emph{Goal:} G3.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_NoHarmfulComparison}:] \progname{} shall not make users feel that they are falling far behind other users.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH6. \emph{Evidence:} Comparison is fine when slightly behind, and ``only ever bad when you're really far behind'' (P02). \emph{Goal:} G3.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_ProtectData}:] Users' personal and health data shall be collected only where it is needed, kept secure, and never sold.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH9. \emph{Evidence:} One participant avoided step counters from large companies, turned off their step counter's network access, and keeps their portfolio records on their own computer (P01); the other shares data only with a reputable company that keeps it secure (P02). Required by PIPEDA~\citep{PIPEDA}. \emph{Goal:} G3.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_ExplainPermissions}:] Users shall understand why each permission is needed before they grant it.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH7. \emph{Evidence:} Both participants grant a permission when there is a clear reason for it (P01, P02), and distrust apps that ask for ``12 permissions'' when two would do (P01). \emph{Goal:} G2, G3.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_ControlOwnData}:] Users shall be able to withdraw their consent and delete their account and data.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH9. \emph{Evidence:} Users' right to withdraw consent is required by PIPEDA~\citep{PIPEDA}. \emph{Goal:} G3.
  \end{itemize}
  \par}

\paragraph{Access and operation}

{\sloppy
  \noindent\begin{itemize}
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_EasyStart}:] Users shall be able to install \progname{} from their phone's app store and start earning rewards without outside help.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH2. \emph{Evidence:} First-time users must complete the core tasks without outside help (Problem Statement Goal~2); how well an app is designed affects whether users trust it (P02). \emph{Goal:} G2.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_PhoneOnly}:] Users shall be able to use every core feature with only their phone, on either Android or iOS, without a wearable device.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH2. \emph{Evidence:} One participant uses an Android phone and no longer wears a fitness band (P01); the other uses an iPhone and an Apple Watch (P02). \emph{Goal:} G2, G5.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_Accessible}:] Users with visual or motor disabilities shall be able to use the core features.\\
          \emph{Source:} SH1, SH6, SH9. \emph{Evidence:} Accessibility is part of serving every user and is expected by app stores (WCAG~2.2~\citep{WCAG22}). \emph{Goal:} G2.
    \item[SR\refstepcounter{srnum}\thesrnum\label{SR_UpdateContent}:] Administrators shall be able to update the tradable assets and the educational content without releasing a new version of the app.\\

The personas below represent the main kinds of user \progname{} is built for.
They are fictional, but each is based on the evidence named in its
\emph{Based on} entry: Personas~1 and~2 on the two interviews (P01 and P02),
and Persona~3 on the target users in the Problem Statement, since neither
interviewee was a beginner at investing. Each persona combines needs from that
evidence with invented details, so no persona describes a real person. Each
persona lists the stakeholder requirements
that matter most to them, so the design can be checked against the people it
serves.

\subsection{Persona 1: Sam}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Name:} Sam, 27, works full time.
  \item \textbf{Description:} Walks most days and has tracked steps on an
        Android phone for years. A long streak at a fixed step goal sometimes
        gets Sam out walking late in the evening. Sam also plays recreational
        sports that the phone does not track, and prefers not to wear a fitness
        band. Sam taught themselves investing from free online content and
        prefers simple, diversified index investing.
  \item \textbf{Goals:} Keep the streak going without it taking over the
        evening; get credit for sports, not only steps; keep learning investing in a
        way that fits their own situation; keep control of their data.
  \item \textbf{Frustrations:} Apps that must be started and stopped by hand;
        investing content that pushes risky stock-picking; apps that ask for far more
        permissions than they need or that may sell their data.
  \item \textbf{Technical Ability:} High. Sam chooses apps for privacy and
        limits what each app can access.
  \item \textbf{Based on:} P01.
  \item \textbf{Most relevant requirements:} \srref{SR_EarnFromActivity},
        \srref{SR_AllAbilities}, \srref{SR_SafeMotivation}, \srref{SR_NotAdvice},
        \srref{SR_ProtectData}, \srref{SR_ExplainPermissions}, \srref{SR_PhoneOnly}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Persona 2: Jordan}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Name:} Jordan, 25, recent graduate.
  \item \textbf{Description:} Studied some finance at school, but only
        understood how to act on it after starting to invest with real money.
        Is active now and then, but exercise is not a priority right now.
        Jordan's phone and smartwatch record their activity, and they check
        their health data out of curiosity. Jordan sets no fitness goals and
        keeps only a rough sense of whether they were active that week.
  \item \textbf{Goals:} Be more consistent with exercise, like a friend Jordan
        admires; learn by doing rather than by watching; understand their account
        options well enough that their savings grow instead of falling behind
        inflation.
  \item \textbf{Frustrations:} Learning from videos that do not stick;
        falling far behind friends; apps that ask for personal data without a clear
        reason.
  \item \textbf{Technical Ability:} Moderate to high. Uses a phone and smartwatch
        daily, and judges whether to trust an app by who makes it and how well it is
        designed.
  \item \textbf{Based on:} P02.
  \item \textbf{Most relevant requirements:} \srref{SR_UsefulRewards},
        \srref{SR_LearnByDoing}, \srref{SR_CanadianContext},
        \srref{SR_ShareProgress}, \srref{SR_NoHarmfulComparison},
        \srref{SR_OwnGoals}, \srref{SR_ExplainPermissions}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Persona 3: Aisha}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Name:} Aisha, 19, first-year student.
  \item \textbf{Description:} Has never invested and does not know what a stock,
        an exchange-traded fund, or a tax-free savings account is. Her savings sit in
        a bank account. She walks between classes but does not exercise regularly.
        She would like to start investing, but is afraid of losing money she cannot
        afford to lose and has heard that investing means studying company finances.
  \item \textbf{Goals:} Understand the basics of investing in plain language;
        start a fitness habit without being judged; try things without real
        consequences.
  \item \textbf{Frustrations:} Financial jargon; advice that contradicts other
        advice; apps that assume she already knows how they work.
  \item \textbf{Technical Ability:} Everyday smartphone use: she can install an
        app and respond to permission prompts, but has never used a fitness or
        investing app.
  \item \textbf{Based on:} the target users described in the Problem Statement,
        and both interviewees' observations that many people do nothing with their
        savings (P02) and that beginners are put off by the belief that investing
        requires expert analysis (P01). This persona will be validated in usability
        testing.
  \item \textbf{Most relevant requirements:} \srref{SR_RiskFree},
        \srref{SR_LearnInContext}, \srref{SR_CanadianContext}, \srref{SR_EasyStart},
        \srref{SR_AllAbilities}, \srref{SR_SeeProgress}.
\end{itemize}

User

\refstepcounter{ucnum}\label{UC_BuyAsset}
\subsection{\ucref{UC_BuyAsset}: Buy a Simulated Asset}

\textbf{Primary Actor}

User

\textbf{Preconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The user has enough virtual currency for the purchase.
\item A usable market price is available.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Main Success Scenario}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The user selects an asset to purchase.
\item The user enters the quantity.
\item The system displays the total cost.
\item The user confirms the transaction.
\item The system updates the balance and holdings.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows}
\begin{itemize}
\item If the user has insufficient currency, the purchase is rejected.
\item If the transaction fails, the balance and holdings remain unchanged.
\item If the market price is outdated, the system does not allow the trade and informs the user.
\item If the market is closed, the system queues the trade until the market reopens.
\item If the user submits the same trade twice, the system only processes it once.
\item If this is the user's first trade, they must acknowledge that the market is simulated and does not provide financial advice.
\item If the user chooses to sell instead, the system checks their shares and adds the virtual currency from the sale to their balance.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Postconditions}
\begin{itemize}
\item The purchase is recorded and the portfolio is updated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Simulated Market and Portfolio}

The live market is the simulated market that follows current real-world
prices. A market scenario replays historical prices instead, with its own
balance and holdings (\rref{FR_MktScenario}). Scenario prices are the replayed
day's closing prices (\rref{FR_MktScenarioAdvance}), so the rules on market
hours and price age (\rref{FR_MktPriceAge}, \rref{FR_MktNoStaleTrade} and
\rref{FR_MktQueueClosed}) apply only to the live market. Every asset is priced in
\texttt{QUOTE\_CURRENCY}, and one unit of virtual currency buys one unit of
\texttt{QUOTE\_CURRENCY} worth of an asset. Section~\ref{SecFormal} defines
trades and portfolio value.

\subsection{Formal Definitions} \label{SecFormal}

This subsection defines the daily reward, the streak, trades and portfolio
value that the requirements in Sections 6.2 and 6.3 refer to. For a user on
calendar day $d$ in their time zone:
\begin{itemize}
\item $T$ is the set of supported activity types
  (\texttt{SUPPORTED\_ACTIVITY\_\allowbreak TYPES}).
\item $x_{t,d}$ is the user's total of activity type $t$ on day $d$, counting
  only plausible records (\rref{FR_ActPlausible}).
\item $u_t$ is the amount of activity type $t$ that earns one unit of virtual
  currency (\texttt{EARNING\_RATES}).
\item $G_d$ is the set of the user's activity goals in effect on day $d$. A
  goal $(t, g)$ is met when $x_{t,d} \geq g$.
\item $\mathit{rest}_d$ is true when the user marked day $d$ as a rest day.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Daily reward.} The virtual currency awarded for day $d$ is
\begin{equation}
R_d = \min\Bigl(\text{\texttt{MAX\_DAILY\_CURRENCY}},\
  \sum_{t \in T} \Bigl\lfloor \frac{x_{t,d}}{u_t} \Bigr\rfloor\Bigr).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Streak.} Day $d$ counts toward the streak when
\begin{equation}
q_d = \mathit{rest}_d \lor \bigl(G_d \neq \emptyset \land
  \forall (t, g) \in G_d : x_{t,d} \geq g\bigr) \lor
  \Bigl(G_d = \emptyset \land \sum_{t \in T} x_{t,d} > 0\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The streak at the end of day $d$ is $s_d = s_{d-1} + 1$ if $q_d$ is true,
and $s_d = 0$ otherwise, with $s = 0$ before the user's first day.

\paragraph{Trades.} Let $B$ be the user's balance, $h_a$ the number of shares
of asset $a$ they hold, and $p_a$ the price at which a trade of $a$ executes.
A purchase of $n$ shares, where $n$ is a positive integer, is accepted only if
$n \cdot p_a \leq B$, and then sets $B \gets B - n \cdot p_a$ and
$h_a \gets h_a + n$. A sale of $n$ shares, where $n$ is a positive integer, is accepted only if $n \leq h_a$, and
then sets $B \gets B + n \cdot p_a$ and $h_a \gets h_a - n$.

\paragraph{Portfolio value.} With $p_a$ the most recent price of asset $a$, the
portfolio value is
\begin{equation}
V = \sum_{a} h_a \cdot p_a.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Privacy and Data Retention}
\label{SecPrivacyRetention}

This subsection restates the Hazard Analysis requirements that protect the
data \progname{} collects, which is listed in Section~\ref{SecDataDictionary},
and keep it correct. Each one keeps its Hazard Analysis identifier and
wording. The other Hazard Analysis requirements appear in Section~\ref{SecFR},
except SAF-5, which applies only if goal suggestions are added, and SEC-36,
which is split between \rref{FR_SocialAccept} and \rref{FR_SocialRemove}.

\begin{description}

\item[SEC-7 (Least-privilege health access).] The system shall request read access only to the health data types needed for
the supported activity types (SRS Appendix~C,
\texttt{SUPPORTED\_ACTIVITY\_TYPES}), and shall not request permission to
write to the health platform.

\textit{Fit criterion:} Every health permission the system requests is a read
permission for a data type needed by one of the supported activity types.

\item[SEC-8 (State the purpose of each permission).] Before requesting each permission, the system shall tell the user which
feature uses that data.

\textit{Fit criterion:} For every permission request, a test user is shown the
feature that uses that data before the request appears.

\item[SEC-9 (Consent and withdrawal).] The system shall obtain the user's consent before it first reads their health
data, and shall stop reading health data when the user withdraws consent in
the app.

\textit{Fit criterion:} A new test user's health data is not read until they
consent. After they withdraw consent in the app, no further health data is
read.

\item[SEC-10 (Data minimization).] The system shall send activity data from the user's mobile device to its
server only as daily totals for each activity type, not as individual activity
records.

\textit{Fit criterion:} The activity data a test phone sends to the server
contains only daily totals for each activity type and no individual activity
records.

\item[SEC-11 (No data to third parties).] The system shall not send users' personal or health data to any third party,
other than a user's email address and password reset link to the email service
that delivers it. Requests to the market data provider shall identify assets
only by symbol.

\textit{Fit criterion:} Every outgoing request from a test mobile device and
from the server goes to \progname{}'s own server, the market data provider or
the email service. Requests to the market data provider contain no personal or
health data and identify assets only by symbol. Requests to the email service
contain no personal or health data other than the user's email address and
their password reset link.

\item[SEC-12 (No location data).] The system shall not read or store location or route data.

\textit{Fit criterion:} The permissions a test build requests include no
location permission, and no location or route data is stored on the phone or
the server.

\item[SEC-13 (Private by default).] The system shall show a user's statistics only to that user until they choose
to share them, and shall show a shared update only to the friends the user
selected for it. When the user deletes a shared update or removes a friend,
the system shall stop showing the affected shared updates to that friend.

\textit{Fit criterion:} A test user's new statistic is visible to no other
user. Once shared with selected friends, it is visible to exactly those
friends. After the user deletes the shared update or removes one of those
friends, the shared update no longer appears in that friend's social feed.

\item[SEC-14 (No location detail in shared updates).] The system shall not include location, route or the exact time an activity
took place in shared updates.

\textit{Fit criterion:} No shared update shown to a test user's friends
contains a location, a route, or the exact time an activity took place.

\item[SEC-15 (Authenticated access only).] The system shall require an authenticated session for every request that reads
or changes a user's data, and shall return another user's data, other than
their username, only when that user has shared it with the requester.

\textit{Fit criterion:} A test request without a valid session is rejected. A
request for another user's data, other than their username, that was not
shared with the requester is rejected, and a request for data that was shared
succeeds.

\item[SEC-16 (Protect data in transit and at rest).] The system shall encrypt user data in transit between the phone and the
server, and when stored on the server, including in backups.

\textit{Fit criterion:} Inspecting the network traffic between a test phone
and the server, the data stored on the server, and its backups shows no
readable user data.

\item[SEC-17 (Session expiry).] The system shall end a session when the user signs out of it or when it has
not been used for \texttt{SESSION\_TIMEOUT}, after which the user must sign in
again.

\textit{Fit criterion:} A test session is rejected after the user signs out of
it, and after it is left unused for longer than \texttt{SESSION\_TIMEOUT}. In
both cases the user must sign in again to continue.

\item[SEC-18 (In-app account deletion).] The system shall let the user delete their account from within the app, and
shall delete all data it stores about them, as listed in the SRS data
dictionary (SRS Section~7.2), from the server and its backups within
\texttt{DATA\_DELETION\_PERIOD} of the request.

\textit{Fit criterion:} After a test user deletes their account in the app,
they can no longer sign in. Within \texttt{DATA\_DELETION\_PERIOD}, neither
the server nor its backups hold any data about them listed in the SRS data
dictionary, including their account details, profile, activity totals, goals,
rest days, reminder settings, consent records, currency balance, holdings,
trades, scenario portfolios, learning progress, shared updates, friend list
and friend requests, and none of their shared updates appear in friends'
social feeds.

\item[SEC-19 (Count each activity once).] The system shall award currency for each activity record at most once, even
when the record is read more than once.

\textit{Fit criterion:} When the same test records are read on two consecutive
syncs, the user is rewarded for them once.

\item[SEC-20 (Catch late activity).] On each sync, the system shall read the activity records of the last
\texttt{SYNC\_LOOKBACK\_PERIOD} and reward any record that has not already
been rewarded.

\textit{Fit criterion:} A test record written to the health platform two days
after the activity took place is rewarded on the next sync, once.

\item[SEC-21 (Activity unavailable).] When the system cannot read activity data, because permission has not been
granted or the health platform does not respond, it shall tell the user that
activity could not be read, keep the currency already earned, and keep every
feature that does not need new activity data usable.

\textit{Fit criterion:} With health permission denied, and separately with the
health platform returning errors, a test user is told that activity could not
be read, their balance is unchanged, and trading, learning and the social feed
still work.

\item[SEC-22 (Balance history).] The system shall record every change to a user's currency balance with its
amount, time and reason, and the balance shall always equal the sum of its
recorded changes.

\textit{Fit criterion:} For a test user after a series of rewards and trades,
the balance equals the sum of the recorded changes, and each change shows its
amount, time and reason.

\item[SEC-23 (Count activity on the user's day).] The system shall assign each activity record to a calendar day using the
user's time zone at the time of the activity.

\textit{Fit criterion:} Test records at 23:30 and 00:30 local time are
assigned to two different days, and a record made before a time zone change
keeps the day on which it took place.

\item[SEC-24 (The server decides rewards and balances).] The system shall calculate rewards, balances and holdings only on the server,
from the daily activity totals the app sends, and shall reject any reward,
balance or holding reported by the app and any daily total dated after
the server's current date in the user's time zone.

\textit{Fit criterion:} A test request from a modified app that reports its
own reward, balance or holding is rejected, and a test daily total dated after
the server's current date in the user's time zone is rejected and earns nothing.

\item[SEC-25 (Validate every request).] The server shall reject any request whose inputs are missing, of the wrong
type, or outside the limits stated in the SRS.

\textit{Fit criterion:} Test requests with a missing field, a value of the
wrong type, and a value outside its SRS limit, such as a negative trade
quantity, are each rejected with an error and change no data.

\item[SEC-26 (Backups).] The system shall back up all persistent data at least once every
\texttt{BACKUP\_INTERVAL}, and restoring from a backup shall be tested before
each release.

\textit{Fit criterion:} The backup log shows a backup in every
\texttt{BACKUP\_INTERVAL}, and the release checklist shows a successful test
restore before each release.

\item[SEC-27 (Prices in one currency).] The system shall store every price with its currency, and shall not use a
price that is not in \texttt{QUOTE\_CURRENCY}.

\textit{Fit criterion:} Every stored test price has a currency. A test price
received in a currency other than \texttt{QUOTE\_CURRENCY} is not shown or
used in any balance, trade or portfolio value.

\item[SEC-28 (Apply stock splits).] When an asset undergoes a stock split, the system shall adjust users' holdings
and the asset's price history so that the split does not change any portfolio
value.

\textit{Fit criterion:} For a test asset that splits 2-for-1, every holding
doubles, every historical price halves, and every portfolio value is the same
before and after.

\item[SEC-29 (Trade at the confirmed price).] The system shall execute a trade made while the asset's market is open at the
price shown when the user confirmed it. If that price is no longer the latest
price, it shall show the user the new price and ask them to confirm again.

\textit{Fit criterion:} While a test asset's market is open, a trade confirmed
at a price that has not changed executes at that price. When the price changes
between viewing and confirming, the trade does not execute until the user
confirms the new price.

\item[SEC-30 (Accurate values).] The system shall calculate every displayed balance and portfolio value to
within \texttt{VALUE\_TOLERANCE} of its exact value.

\textit{Fit criterion:} For test portfolios with hand-calculated values, every
displayed balance and portfolio value differs from the hand-calculated value
by at most \texttt{VALUE\_TOLERANCE}.

\item[SEC-31 (Separate scenario portfolios).] The system shall keep each market scenario's portfolio and balance separate
from the user's live portfolio and balance.

\textit{Fit criterion:} After a test user buys and sells in a scenario, their
live balance and holdings are unchanged.

\item[SEC-32 (Complete scenario data).] The system shall offer a market scenario only after its historical data has
been checked to contain a price for every asset in it on every trading day of
its period.

\textit{Fit criterion:} The check is run on every scenario dataset before
release, and a test dataset with one missing day is not offered.

\item[SEC-33 (Provider credentials stay on the server).] The system shall store the credentials for every external service only on the
server, never in the app or in the code repository.

\textit{Fit criterion:} Inspection of the app package and an automated scan of
the repository find no external service credentials.

\item[SEC-34 (Private notifications).] The system shall not include health data, balances or portfolio values in a
notification unless the user has turned this on.

\textit{Fit criterion:} With default settings, no notification sent to a test
phone contains health data, a balance or a portfolio value.

\item[SEC-38 (Limit sign-in attempts).] The system shall require passwords of at least \texttt{MIN\_PASSWORD\_LENGTH}
characters, and after \texttt{MAX\_FAILED\_SIGNINS} failed sign-in attempts on
an account shall refuse further attempts on it for
\texttt{SIGNIN\_LOCKOUT\_PERIOD}.

\textit{Fit criterion:} A test password shorter than
\texttt{MIN\_PASSWORD\_LENGTH} is refused at sign-up, and after
\texttt{MAX\_FAILED\_SIGNINS} wrong passwords even the correct password is
refused until \texttt{SIGNIN\_LOCKOUT\_PERIOD} has passed.

\item[SEC-39 (Protect the session on the phone).] The system shall store the user's session only in the phone's secure storage.

\textit{Fit criterion:} Inspection of a test phone's app storage finds the
session only in the operating system's secure storage.

\end{description}

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}
\noindent 
\begin{itemize}

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Accuracy}:]
  \textbf{Accuracy}

  The system shall calculate virtual currency balances and simulated portfolio
  values within \texttt{VALUE\_TOLERANCE} of their expected values.

  \textit{Fit criterion:} Independently calculated test cases differ from the
  displayed result by no more than \texttt{VALUE\_TOLERANCE}.

  \textit{Traces to:} G4, G5,
  \rref{FR_ActEarn}, \rref{FR_MktBuy},
  \rref{FR_LearnTradeEffect}, Hazard Analysis SEC-30.

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Usability}:]
  \textbf{Usability}

  At least \texttt{MIN\_TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE} of first-time users shall be able
  to complete the core activity and simulated investing tasks without outside
  assistance.

  \textit{Fit criterion:} At least \texttt{MIN\_TASK\_SUCCESS\_RATE} of
  usability test participants complete the core tasks independently.

  \textit{Traces to:} G1, G2,
  \rref{FR_ActEarn}, \rref{FR_MktBuy},
  \rref{FR_LearnKnowledgeCheck}.

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Maintainability}:]
  \textbf{Maintainability}

  The effort required to implement a likely change shall not exceed
  \texttt{MAX\_CHANGE\_EFFORT} of the original development effort for the
  affected feature.

  \textit{Fit criterion:} A modification requires no more than
  \texttt{MAX\_CHANGE\_EFFORT} of the original feature's development hours.

  \textit{Traces to:} G1, G4, G5,
  \rref{FR_LearnUpdateContent}.

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Portability}:]
  \textbf{Portability}

  The core features of \progname{} shall operate on supported Android and iOS
  devices.

  \textit{Fit criterion:} Core acceptance tests pass on both supported
  operating systems.

  \textit{Traces to:} G2, G3, G4, G5,
  \scref{SC_Platform}, \scref{SC_HealthPlatform}.

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Performance}:]
  \textbf{Performance}

  The system shall display previously loaded activity summaries, portfolio
  summaries and social feed updates
  within \texttt{MAX\_RESPONSE\_TIME} of the user's request in at least
  \texttt{MIN\_RESPONSE\_RATE} of requests.

  \textit{Fit criterion:} At least \texttt{MIN\_RESPONSE\_RATE} of
  \texttt{RESPONSE\_TRIALS} timed trials complete within
  \texttt{MAX\_RESPONSE\_TIME} under a stable network connection.

  \textit{Traces to:} G2,
  \rref{FR_ActTrends}, \rref{FR_MktPortfolioValue},
  \rref{FR_SocialFeed}.

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Reliability}:]
  \textbf{Reliability}

  The system shall preserve currency balances and portfolio holdings when
  trades are interrupted or repeated.

  \textit{Fit criterion:} In \texttt{RELIABILITY\_TRIALS} interruption and
  retry tests, no duplicate or incomplete trades are recorded.

  \textit{Traces to:} G4,
  \rref{FR_MktAtomicTrade}, \rref{FR_MktNoDuplicate},
  Hazard Analysis SEC-5 and SEC-6.

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Security}:]
  \textbf{Security}

  The system shall prevent unauthorized access to
  private user information.

  \textit{Fit criterion:} All tested requests without
  valid authentication or authorization are rejected.

  \textit{Traces to:} G3,
  \rref{FR_AcctSignIn}, \rref{FR_AcctSignOut},
  \rref{FR_SocialChooseFriends},
  Hazard Analysis SEC-15 and SEC-17.

\item[NFR\refstepcounter{nfrnum}\thenfrnum \label{NFR_Accessibility}:]
  \textbf{Accessibility}

  The core features shall remain usable when the device text size is increased
  to \texttt{MAX\_TEXT\_SCALE} of its default size.

  \textit{Fit criterion:} Users can read essential information and complete
  core tasks at \texttt{MAX\_TEXT\_SCALE} text scaling without clipped
  controls.

  \textit{Traces to:} G2,
  \rref{FR_AcctCreate}, \rref{FR_MktBuy},
  \rref{FR_LearnKnowledgeCheck},
  \rref{FR_SocialShare}.

\end{itemize}

\section{Rationale} \label{SecRationale}

This section explains the reasoning behind the major requirements
and decisions made for \progname{}.

\subsection{Simulated Investing}

\progname{} uses virtual currency instead of real money so users
can learn about investing without the financial risk associated with investing. This allows
beginners to experiment with different investment decisions
without losing any real savings. The restriction on real money
transactions also keeps the system focused on education rather
than financial services (\scref{SC_NoRealMoney},
\rref{FR_MktBuy}).

\subsection{Activity Based Rewards}

The system rewards users for everyday physical activity to
encourage consistent exercise habits. Rather than requiring the user to do
specific individual workouts, the app uses activity that is already recorded by
the user's phone health platform. A daily earning limit is
enforced to discourage excessive exercise for virtual
rewards (\scref{SC_HealthPlatform},
\rref{FR_ActEarn}, \rref{FR_ActDailyCap}).

\subsection{Goals and Streaks}

Users can set their own activity goals and track their streaks
to stay motivated. Goals are optional because not every user
wants to follow a specific activity target. Rest days are also
included so users do not feel pressured to exercise every day
to maintain their streak. Missing a goal does not reduce
the user's currency or holdings
(\rref{FR_ActGoal}, \rref{FR_ActGoalOptional},
\rref{FR_ActRestDay}, \rref{FR_ActNoPenalty}).

\subsection{Financial Education}

Educational explanations and knowledge checks are included
because simulated trading alone may not help beginners
understand their investment decisions. By providing explanations
alongside market activity, users can learn financial concepts
while practising them. Portfolio feedback also helps users
understand the effects of their trades
(\rref{FR_LearnInPlace},
\rref{FR_LearnTradeEffect},
\rref{FR_LearnKnowledgeCheck}).

\subsection{Real-World Market Data}

The simulated market uses real-world prices to give users
a more realistic investing experience. However, outdated
prices could create unrealistic trading results. The system
therefore prevents trades using stale prices while the market
is open and queues trades requested while the market is
closed (\scref{SC_MarketData},
\rref{FR_MktRealPrices},
\rref{FR_MktNoStaleTrade},
\rref{FR_MktQueueClosed}).

Trades must also be recorded correctly to make sure users
do not lose virtual currency or receive incorrect holdings.
The system prevents duplicate trades and ensures that both
the user's balance and holdings are updated together
(\rref{FR_MktAtomicTrade},
\rref{FR_MktNoDuplicate}).

\subsection{Privacy and Social Features}

The social features are intended to motivate users without
requiring them to share private information. Users choose
which friends can view their updates, and personal body
measurements are excluded from shared content. Users can
also remove friends or delete shared updates to maintain
control over their information
(\rref{FR_SocialChooseFriends},
\rref{FR_SocialNoBody},
\rref{FR_SocialRemove},
\rref{FR_SocialDeleteUpdate},
\nfrref{NFR_Security}).

The system also limits the health information it collects
and does not read or store location data. This helps protect
users from having sensitive personal information exposed.
Health data is only accessed with consent, and users can
withdraw that consent when they choose (\rref{FR_SetWithdrawConsent},
Hazard Analysis SEC-9 and SEC-12).


\subsection{Security and Data Protection}

Since the system stores personal information, activity totals,
and simulated investment records, protecting this data is
important. Users must be signed in before accessing private
information, and their data must be protected during storage
and transmission. Sessions also expire to reduce the risk of
unauthorized access (\nfrref{NFR_Security}, Hazard Analysis SEC-15 to
SEC-17).

\subsection{Usability and Accessibility}

Because \progname{} is intended for beginners, users should not need previous
investing knowledge, fitness experience or specialized fitness equipment. The
main features are designed to be easy to understand, and they support
different levels of physical activity and the phone's accessibility settings
(\nfrref{NFR_Usability}, \nfrref{NFR_Portability},
\nfrref{NFR_Accessibility}).

\section{Likely Changes} 

The following changes are identified in the project documents. They describe parts of FitFolio that may change as the team tests its planned integrations or adds features already listed as stretch goals.

\noindent \begin{itemize}
\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_futureChange}:]
\textbf{Activity-data updates}

The timing of \progname{}'s activity-data updates may change because of restrictions imposed by the phone's health platform. The Development Plan identifies updating activity data when the user opens the app as an alternative if background updates cannot work reliably. This would affect when activity data is retrieved in \ucref{UC_RecordActivity} (Record Daily Activity) and becomes available for awarding currency under \rref{FR_ActEarn}. Any change must still follow the health-platform access rules in SC\ref{SC_HealthAccess}.


\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_secondChange}:]
\textbf{Wearable device support}

FitFolio may be extended to support wearable devices, such as the Apple Watch and other compatible fitness trackers. Wearable integration is already listed as a stretch goal in the Problem Statement and Goals. Adding this support could provide additional activity data, including workout duration and calories burned. This would affect the Activity Record entity in Table~\ref{tab:MajorDataEntities} and its fields in Table~\ref{tab:DataDictionary}. Direct access to a wearable, rather than reading its data through the phone's health platform, would also require revising SC\ref{SC_HealthPlatform}. Wearables would remain optional, as required by \srref{SR_PhoneOnly}.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_thirdChange}:]
\textbf{Market-data access}

The market-data provider, price-update frequency, and selection of supported assets may change if the chosen service's request limits or costs prevent the planned use. The Development Plan identifies switching providers, reducing the update frequency, and limiting the asset list as possible responses. These changes would affect the provider choice in SC\ref{SC_MarketData}, the prices obtained under \rref{FR_MktRealPrices}, and the available assets represented by the Market Data entity in Table~\ref{tab:MajorDataEntities}. Changes to the update frequency would also need to account for the price-age display in \rref{FR_MktPriceAge} and the restriction on trading with stale prices in \rref{FR_MktNoStaleTrade}. Any reduction below the price-freshness or asset-coverage targets in Section~\ref{SecProductVision} would require an agreed revision to those targets and the affected requirements.

\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_fourthChange}:] 
\textbf{Historical market scenarios}

The number and selection of historical market scenarios may change depending on the availability and cost of historical price data. The Development Plan identifies offering fewer scenarios or using a selected set of stored historical datasets as possible responses if the required data is difficult to obtain. This would affect the past market conditions available under \srref{SR_PastMarkets} and the historical prices represented by the Market Data entity in Table~\ref{tab:MajorDataEntities} and its \texttt{price\_history} field in Table~\ref{tab:DataDictionary}.


\item[LC\refstepcounter{lcnum}\thelcnum\label{LC_5thChange}:] 
\textbf{Social challenges and leaderboards}

FitFolio may be extended to include fitness challenges and opt-in leaderboards. Social challenges and leaderboards are listed as a stretch goal in the Problem Statement and Goals, and the Hazard Analysis identifies opt-in leaderboards as a possible future feature. The current version prohibits ranking users by activity, currency, or portfolio performance under \rref{FR_SocialNoRanking} and Hazard Analysis SAF-11. Adding leaderboards would therefore require an agreed revision to those requirements and a review of the risk of harmful comparison identified in \srref{SR_NoHarmfulComparison}. Adding comparisons of portfolio statistics would also require revisiting the allowed updates in \rref{FR_SocialShare} and Table~\ref{TblShareable}. Users would still choose whether to participate and which information to share, consistent with \srref{SR_ControlSharing} and \rref{FR_SocialChooseFriends}.


\end{itemize}

\section{Unlikely Changes}

The following parts of FitFolio are expected to stay the same because they support the project's purpose and goals.

\noindent \begin{itemize}
\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_firstChange}:]
\textbf{Virtual currency and simulated investing}

FitFolio is expected to continue using virtual currency with no real-world monetary value. Adding real-money trading or allowing users to exchange earned currency for cash is unlikely. The Problem Statement and Goals describes an app where beginners can practise investing without risking real money or needing to link bank or brokerage accounts. Moving to real money would change this purpose.

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_SecondChange}:]
\textbf{Earning rewards through physical activity}

Physical activity is expected to remain the way users earn virtual currency in FitFolio. Removing activity-based rewards is unlikely because earning currency through exercise and using it in the simulated market is the project's central idea. This connection supports the goal of helping users build fitness habits while learning about investing. 

\item[ULC\refstepcounter{ulcnum}\theulcnum\label{ULC_thirdChange}:]
\textbf{User control over social sharing}

Users are expected to keep control over what they share and with which friends. Removing this control is unlikely because the Problem Statement and Goals includes motivating users through friends while protecting their privacy. Only the kinds of update in Table~\ref{TblShareable} can be shared, and raw health data and portfolio values are never shared (\rref{FR_SocialShare}, \rref{FR_SocialNoBody}). Any changes to the social features should preserve this.


\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{1. What went well while writing this deliverable?}

\textbf{Team:} What went well was grounding the requirements in evidence
instead of in our own assumptions. Before writing, we interviewed two
potential users, and every stakeholder requirement in Section~3 records its
source stakeholder and the evidence behind it, so a reader can see why each
one exists. Splitting the document through GitHub issues also worked: each
section had one owner, and shared conventions, such as one format for every
functional requirement, mnemonic labels, and a single appendix for symbolic
constants, kept five people's writing consistent. Reviewing each other's pull
requests caught real problems before submission, such as a shared table that
contradicted a Hazard Analysis requirement and two different words used for
the same idea. Finally, because every requirement has a label, traceability
became a by-product of writing: each functional requirement names the
stakeholder requirement, use case and goal it comes from, which made the
traceability matrices straightforward to build.
